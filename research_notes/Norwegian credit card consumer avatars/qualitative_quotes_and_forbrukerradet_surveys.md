### Inferences
- The adviser accounts describe two distinct debt-entry pathways that map well onto avatars:
  - **"Fall-from-normal"**: an established household hit by divorce, illness or job loss maxes out cards to keep up appearances (A1, A2, A8).
  - **"Never-learned / low-friction credit"**: younger or low-resource people for whom clicking for credit is easier than the welfare office (A6, A20), who treat a card limit as a buffer (A3), or who don't see BNPL as credit (A14–A17).
- Shame plus avoidance (letterbox anxiety, years of secrecy, fear of judgement) is the strongest recurring emotional theme (A7–A10). Messaging that is non-judgemental ("ikke døm meg") is directly supported by A9.
- These are professional narratives about a selected, high-severity clientele who are often already NAV clients. They should not be generalised to typical card holders. Most card holders carry small balances (Q5).

## Q5. Forbrukerrådet *Bytteundersøkelsen* 2023–2026: switching/renegotiation, card and consumer-loan switching, Finansportalen awareness and use, reasons for not switching, new credit agreements, and consumers' own words

### Takeaway
All four editions were read in full (VERIFIED). Shares per year, among users of each product:

| Product | Moved or renegotiated (data years 2023 / 2024 / 2025) |
|---|---|
| Credit cards | 9% / 13% / 9% |
| Consumer loans | 18% / 25% / 20% |
| Mortgages | 27% / 27% / 32% |

- Card renegotiation within the same bank is rare (2% each year).
- Finansportalen awareness fell from about 41–43% (2019–2022) to 25–27% after a 2023–2024 name change. It is lowest among 18–24-year-olds (12–15%) and women (19–20%).
- About 15% of bank-service switchers used Finansportalen in 2025.
- In 2025, 20% opened a new credit agreement: 7% a card or store account, 11% BNPL, 5% a car loan, 1% a consumer loan.
- Asked why they opened a new credit card or consumer loan, people cite "praktisk" (practical, 48% in 2025) and travel/shop offers (56–59% in earlier years).
- Most card holders report outstanding card debt under NOK 20,000 (81–85%). Consumer-loan holders carry much larger sums.

### Inferences
- **Credit-card switching is low and mostly means changing provider, not negotiating.** About 8–11% move a card in a year, and only 2% renegotiate. This fits a product chosen once (often bundled with a bank switch, a job, a loyalty scheme or Apple Pay) and rarely revisited. The open-ended answers show cards being acquired incidentally: "anbefalte å opprette kredittkort i samme slengen", "koblet opp med ny jobb", "Trumf", "Coop byttet leverandør".
- **Consumers' own reasons are mostly practical and transactional, not about borrowing.** Common reasons: practicality, travel, travel insurance, safe online shopping, bonus and loyalty schemes, a spare card. Some answers do reveal a credit or liquidity need: "Enkel tilgang på ekstra likviditet", "Samlet småkreditt på ett sted", "Depositum for husleie", "Fordi jeg ikke hadde penger på konto" (12%).
- **Finansportalen reaches a minority**: about 1 in 4 adults are aware of it, and only 12–15% of 18–24-year-olds. Comparison behaviour runs mostly through the bank itself (55%) rather than neutral comparison tools. Third-party card-comparison sites are marginal in this survey (kredittkort.com 1%).
- **Inertia is driven mainly by perceived satisfaction and small perceived gains**: "fornøyd" 48%, "ikke så mye å spare" 26%. Friction ("for krevende og komplisert" 12%, "tar for mye tid" 9%) is secondary.

### Gaps
- The Bytte surveys do not ask about payment behaviour on cards (full vs minimum), the number of cards held, credit limits, or interest-rate awareness.
- The open-ended answers shown are Forbrukerrådet's selection, not the full set.

## Q6. Other Forbrukerrådet credit material: the 2015 10th-grader card survey, the NAV-adviser report's recommendations, the January 2026 policy input

### Takeaway
- **2015 Norstat survey of 300 tenth-graders** (VERIFIED, older data, labelled):
  - 66% knew that paying by credit card means taking a loan; 27% said it does not, and 7% didn't know.
  - Only 11% knew typical card interest is over 20%.
  - 49% expected to get a card at 18. 35% of those didn't know what they would use it for; the rest named mostly everyday consumption.
- **NAV-adviser report**: recommends considering "et forbud mot aktiv markedsføring av kredittkort og forbrukslån", along with better cost information, better credit assessments, scrutiny of Klarna and stronger use of the duty to refuse.
- **January 2026 input**: Forbrukerrådet says most effective card rates on Finansportalen were 27–28%, and proposes a rate cap and a ban on credit marketing on social media.

- p.7 proposal: a rate cap between Finland's 15% and Sweden's 20% plus the policy rate, using effective rate. The text gives "et påslag på eksempelvis 18 prosent effektiv rente på styringsrenten" as an example.
- p.6: on 12.1.2026 the portal listed 146 providers of a NOK 50,000 five-year consumer loan and 182 credit-card providers.
- p.9:
  - The resulting journey is described as: "veien fra impuls til gjeldsopptak kan ta sekunder" (the road from impulse to taking on debt can take seconds).
  - Proposals: a general ban on surveillance-based marketing, and a ban on all credit marketing on social media, including BNPL messaging.
  - Notes that in 2023 a Storting majority asked for a credit-marketing ban, which "foreløpig ikke blitt fulgt opp av regjeringen" (has not yet been followed up by the government).
- — [Forbrukerrådet PDF](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/01/20260114-forbrukerradets-innspill-til-finansmarkedsmeldingen-endelig.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2015 survey shows that, before adulthood, a third of teenagers do not equate card use with borrowing, and fewer than 1 in 8 know real card rates. The 2025–2026 adviser and Forbrukerrådet statements about BNPL ("de tror de betaler med kort", "«ta det på Klarna» som noe annet enn kreditt") suggest the card-vs-credit confusion persists in a new form. This is a continuity of the theme, not a measured trend.
- Teenagers who reject cards mostly cite fear of not being able to repay (29%) or of collection and payment remarks (10%). This is a risk-averse framing that responsible-credit messaging could build on.

### Gaps
- No Forbrukerrådet survey from 2018–2026 on credit-card knowledge among young adults (18–25) was found. The 2015 survey has not been repeated, as far as I could find.
- Forbrukerrådet's årsrapport 2025 (p.61) refers to consumer enquiries about BNPL but gives no counts.

## Q7. Finanstilsynet data on paying credit-card bills in full / above minimum / minimum only

### Takeaway
VERIFIED via a 2016 Finanstilsynet letter: in its pre-2013 survey of invoicing practice, about 50% of customers paid the full balance or more at first invoicing, about 26% paid more than the minimum but not the full balance, and "de øvrige" (the rest, by implication about 24%) paid the minimum. This differs from the earlier pass's snippet figures (46% / 26% / 17%). The original 2013 report now returns 404. No post-2016 figure was found.

### Cited Findings
- Source: Finanstilsynet, *Retningslinjer for å fakturere kredittkort*, identical letter to banks and finance companies, ref. 15/4738, dated 25.05.2016, p.1, section II. VERIFIED. Text: "Finanstilsynet gjennomførte før utarbeidelsen av rundskrivet i 2013 en undersøkelse av praksis rundt fakturering av kreditkortgjeld. … Videre viste undersøkelsen at ca. 50 prosent av kundene betalte all utestående kreditt eller mer (positiv saldo) ved første gangs fakturering, ca. 26 prosent betalte mer enn det avtalte minimumsbeløpet, men ikke totalt utestående, mens de øvrige betalte minimumsbeløpet."
- The same passage says few firms gave the information the Financial Agreements Act requires. Many invoiced with a minimum amount in the payment field, with little if any information on the cost of deferring payment.

- Limitations: industry-reported data on customers' payment at "første gangs fakturering"; the number of issuers and customers is not stated in this letter; pre-2016 regime with minimum-amount defaults.
