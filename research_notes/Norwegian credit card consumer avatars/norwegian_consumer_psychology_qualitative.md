## 1. Financial literacy: what Norwegians understand about interest, effective rate, credit limits, minimum payments and total cost

### Takeaway
There is no verified, recent (2021–2026) Norwegian population measure of item-level understanding of effektiv rente, compound interest, minimum payments or interest-free periods in what I could access. The best available Norwegian evidence covers broad literacy and self-rated knowledge:
- Self-rated knowledge of consumer loans/credit-card loans ranks near the bottom of personal-finance domains (SIFO 2020).
- Men score clearly higher than women on objective tests, and young women lowest (Nyhus 2023, snippet-only).
- 18–20-year-olds know most about saving and interest calculation, but their knowledge sits mid-scale overall (SIFO 2024).
- Older evidence suggests many teenagers do not realise card use is a loan (Forbrukerrådet 2015, snippet-only).

### Cited Findings
- **SIFO (OsloMet), Nan Zou Bakkeli, *Kunnskap om personlig økonomi*, SIFO-rapport 10-2020** [GEN, with a UC/CC item]. OsloMet news 20 Oct 2020.
  - Data collection: not stated on page, c. 2020. Population: Norwegian adults. Method: survey plus focus-group interviews. Sample size: not stated on page.
  - Result: of seven knowledge types ranked by how much informants say they know, "ta opp forbrukslån/kredittkortlån" ("taking out consumer loans/credit-card loans") ranks 6th of 7, above only "investere".
  - Own experience is the most important learning channel, then parents, then digital tools; school comes last of eight channels. Older people rate their own knowledge higher than young people do.
  - "Flere menn enn kvinner mener de er flinke med penger" (more men than women think they are good with money). More men say they learned from their own mistakes; more women say they learned through life phases ("men forskjellene er små", but the differences are small).
  - Limitations: self-rated knowledge only, not a test; secondary summary.
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate confidence. Reason: SIFO's own summary of its report; sample size not shown. — [OsloMet: Slik lærer vi om personlig økonomi](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/slik-lerer-vi-om-personlig-okonomi)
- **SIFO (OsloMet), Krisztina Gyüre & Markus Lynum, *Kunnskap om privatøkonomi blant ungdom*, SIFO-rapport 7-2024; funded by Kredinorstiftelsen** [GEN/UC]. OsloMet news 13 Aug 2024.
  - Population: young people aged 18–20. Method: survey with a knowledge quiz on saving, loans, spending and investing. Sample size: not stated on page.
  - Results: respondents knew most about saving and "beregning av renter" (calculating interest) and least about investing. Overall knowledge was "midt på den skalaen", mid-scale.
  - Two of three say they have not been taught personal finance at school. One in ten has no one (family or friends) to ask for help if they get into trouble. "De færreste" (very few) understand how macro changes such as inflation or higher interest rates affect household finances.
  - Example quiz item: "Forbrukslån har høyere renter enn boliglån" (correct = true). The summary does not give item-level percentages.
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate confidence. — [OsloMet: Unge fra familier med dårlig råd lærer lite om privatøkonomi hjemme](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/unge-fra-familier-med-darlig-rad-lerer-lite-om-privatokonomi-hjemme)
- **University of Agder, Ellen K. Nyhus, *Finansiell kunnskap i den norske befolkningen*, report, June 2023** [GEN].
  - Data: TNS Kantar surveys in 2018 (50 test questions) and 2022 (25-question subset), representative by region, age and gender. Sample size: not seen.
  - Results reported by ScienceNorway: men answered 62% of 50 questions correctly, women 49.5%. Women with higher education scored about the same as men with only primary school or a vocational background. The youngest women scored lowest.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY; ScienceNorway is not allowlisted, so neither the report nor the article was opened), low-moderate confidence. — [ScienceNorway](https://www.sciencenorway.no/economy-finance-gender-differences/women-with-higher-education-know-as-little-about-personal-finance-as-men-with-a-primary-school-education/2282113)
- **Forbrukerrådet, survey of 10th-graders on credit cards, report dated 8 Sep 2015** [CC].
  - Results: "1 av 3 ungdommer i tiendeklasse er ikke klar over" that using a credit card is a loan. "Bare 1 av 10" knew the actual interest-rate level.
  - Limitations: age about 15–16, not adult holders; 2015; method and sample not seen.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY; PDF on non-allowlisted host), low-moderate confidence. — [Forbrukerrådet PDF](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2015/10/20150908-Rapport_10_klassinger_kredittkort.pdf)
- **SIFO, Kempson, Finney & Poppe, *Financial Well-being: a conceptual model and preliminary analysis*, SIFO Project Note 3-2017** [GEN].
  - Data: Norwegian survey, 2016. Method: ten regression models.
  - Results: three behaviours drive financial well-being: active saving, spending restraint, and not borrowing for daily expenses. Locus of control (control beliefs) mattered at all levels, and "knowledge and experience had little or no effect". This is a key nuance for avatars: in Norway, behaviour and control beliefs mattered more than knowledge.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY via University of Bristol PFRC summary), moderate confidence. — [Bristol PFRC page](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/geography/research/pfrc/themes/capability-behaviours-wellbeing/financial-wellbeing-conceptual-model/)
- **Industry, adjacent: DNB news article, 5 Jun 2024, citing an Ipsos survey for DNB** [CC].
  - DNB's consumer economist "opplever" (perceives) that "spesielt mange unge ikke er klar over at renten på kredittkortgjeld er høyere enn på andre lån" (especially many young people are unaware that credit-card interest is higher than on other loans).
  - This is the economist's impression; no survey figure was found.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY, bank source, not allowlisted), low confidence. — [DNB nyheter](https://www.dnb.no/dnbnyheter/no/din-okonomi/en-av-fire-ma-betale-ferien-med-kredittkort)

### Inferences
- INFERRED (moderate): Norwegians may be fairly competent at basic interest arithmetic (school-taught) yet weak on product-level credit evaluation: comparing credit cards with consumer loans, understanding minimum-payment dynamics, consent to debt-register data use. This gap between arithmetic and product judgement is consistent with the SIFO 2019 misconception finding in Section 3.
- INFERRED (moderate): Gender and age differences point to (a) young women with lower measured literacy and (b) men with higher self-rated competence ("flinke med penger"). Overconfidence is possible but not demonstrated in Norwegian data.
- HYPOTHESIS: the "learning by failing" pattern (Bakkeli 2020) implies that a first-time holder's literacy about credit cards is often built *after* a costly episode. Not tested for credit cards specifically.

### Gaps
- No verified Norwegian 2021–2026 item-level data on understanding of effektiv rente, compound interest, minimum payments, interest-free periods (rentefri periode) or total cost of credit. The Finanstilsynet-commissioned "Finansiell kompetanse i Norge" survey named in the brief was not found by search.
- OECD/INFE 2023 adult survey (39 economies): I could not confirm whether Norway took part, and found no Norway score. The 2024 OsloMet page mentions an OECD study of Norwegian 15-year-olds' financial literacy (PISA 2022), but I have no figures.
- Breakdowns by income and education for credit-specific knowledge were not found.

## 2. Why Norwegians want or hold credit cards, versus why they borrow on them

### Takeaway
Norwegian evidence on motives for *holding* a card (security buffer, travel, online purchase protection, rewards) is thin and mostly indirect. Evidence on *uses of card credit* is clearer: travel, consumer goods and unforeseen expenses (SIFO 2019). Consumers appear to prefer cards over consumer loans because cards feel flexible, easy, safe and controllable.

### Cited Findings
- **SIFO (OsloMet), Christian Poppe, *Lånefinansiert forbruk i Norge anno 2019*, SIFO-rapport 13-2019** [CC/UC]. OsloMet news 7 Feb 2020.
  - Data collection: 2019. Population: adults 18–80. Method: survey. Sample size: not stated on page.
  - Uses: "Kredittkort brukes særlig til reiser (44 prosent), kjøp av forbruksvarer (50 prosent) og uforutsette utgifter (30 prosent)". Consumer loans are used for car purchase (29%), consumer goods (28%) and renovation (12%). Overdraft is used mostly for unforeseen expenses.
  - Why cards win over loans: "Et kredittkort er lett å få tak i, brukervennlig og oppleves som trygt" (a credit card is easy to get, user-friendly and is experienced as safe). Poppe believes "fleksibiliteten" (flexibility) makes the card popular.
  - Limitations: the summary does not say whether the percentages refer to all card holders or to those carrying card debt; sample size not shown.
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate confidence. — [OsloMet: Stor forbrukslån-skam i Norge](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)
- **Forbrukerrådet / Norstat, travel-insurance survey, March 2019** [CC-adjacent].
  - "Reiseforsikring gjennom kredittkort" appeared as one category of travel insurance; the share was not visible. Only 2% of those planning a summer holiday abroad travel without insurance.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), low confidence. — [Forbrukerrådet PDF](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2019/06/rapport-reiseforsikring-mars-2019-003.pdf)
- **Forbrukerrådet, *Bytteundersøkelsen 2026*, published May 2026** [CC/UC].
  - 20% of respondents set up new agreements for forbrukslån, billån, kredittkort or "utsatt betaling" during 2025.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), low-moderate confidence. — [Forbrukerrådet PDF](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)
- **Finanstilsynet, *Rapport om utviklingen i forbruksgjeld*, April 2025** [CC/UC; register data from Gjeldsregisteret, not psychology].
  - Non-interest-bearing debt ("består i hovedsak av kredittkortgjeld som ikke har forfalt", mainly credit-card debt not yet due) grew 13.6% year on year. "80,4 prosent av den totale gjelden var rentebærende ved utgangen av mars 2025."
  - Status: VERIFIED (primary PDF opened), high confidence for the figures. — [Finanstilsynet PDF](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/4990ef/contentassets/aaf5f43e56c8450b97ba4a50190679eb/rapport-om-utviklingen-i-forbruksgjeld-april-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- INFERRED (moderate): holding motives and borrowing motives should be separated in avatars.
  - A transactor/holder motive cluster (travel, everyday purchases, unforeseen expenses as a "buffer") is supported by the SIFO usage pattern and by the large non-interest-bearing card volume in Gjeldsregisteret.
  - A borrowing motive cluster rests on flexibility and the perceived safety and control of cards compared with fixed-instalment loans.
- INFERRED (moderate): "oppleves som trygt" (feels safe) is the core emotional reason to hold a card. It is an emotional benefit, not a cost-based one.

### Gaps
- No Norwegian survey found that measures motives for *holding* a card: security/backup, purchase protection under finansavtaleloven, travel insurance, rewards/bonus, habit, bank offers.
- No Norwegian evidence on rewards/bonus motivation, or on confusion between rewards and costs.
- Industry sources such as Finans Norge and bank surveys may exist but were not located on allowlisted pages.

## 3. What Norwegians misunderstand or struggle to evaluate

### Takeaway
The strongest verified Norwegian misconception is the "forbrukslån-paradokset" (consumer-loan paradox). Many believe consumer loans are more expensive and less controllable than credit-card credit, when the reverse is usually true. Paying only the minimum on several cards is a recognised but hidden marker of vulnerability. Regulators act on the assumption that the default amount shown on an invoice steers repayment.

### Cited Findings
- **SIFO 13-2019 (Poppe), OsloMet summary 7 Feb 2020** [CC/UC].
  - "Mange i undersøkelsen mener dessuten at forbrukslån både er dyrere og mindre kontrollerbart enn kredittkort" (many in the survey believe consumer loans are both more expensive and less controllable than credit cards).
  - "I stedet for å ta opp forbrukslån velger mange heller et mye dyrere kredittkortlån" (instead of a consumer loan, many choose a much more expensive credit-card loan).
  - Poppe "mistenker også lite kunnskap i befolkningen om hva de ulike lånene innebærer" (suspects little knowledge of what the different loans involve). This is his suspicion, not a measured result.
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate-high confidence. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)
- **SIFO 13-2019 on minimum payments and multiple cards** [CC].
  - About 11% of the population is "økonomisk utsatte" (financially vulnerable), meaning they have problems handling at least one of five products. Measuring vulnerability per product type, including minimum-only payment on several cards, reveals "et større problembilde" (a bigger problem picture) than earlier measures.
  - Those with both a consumer loan and credit-card debt are especially exposed.
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate confidence. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)
- **Finanstilsynet, *Rundskriv 10/2016: Retningslinjer for å fakturere kredittkort*, 25 May 2016** (replaces 6/2013) [CC].
  - "På alle fakturasystem skal beløpsfeltet på fakturaen vise samla uteståande kreditt" (the amount field must show the total outstanding balance). The customer must be told the amount can be lowered and what the minimum is. Every invoice with an outstanding balance must include "ein tabell som viser kostnadene ved ulike betalingsløysingar" (a table of costs for different repayment options).
  - Credit limits must not be raised without the customer applying.
  - Status: VERIFIED (primary PDF opened), high confidence. This is policy, not a measured consumer outcome. — [Finanstilsynet PDF](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/contentassets/6ee34941605a4fdea9566bd9b5ecd330/rundskriv10_2016_fakturering_av_kredittkort.pdf)
- **Finanstilsynet 2013 credit-card survey, background to Rundskriv 6/2013** [CC].
  - About 46% paid the total or more, about 26% paid more than the minimum but less than the total, and about 17% paid only the minimum.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY; the PDF returned 404 when fetched), low-moderate confidence. — [Finanstilsynet (404 at fetch)](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/contentassets/1a55528c548d42e0b0cb32d1e09ab480/rapport-kredittkort-05-2013.pdf)
- **NHH master's thesis, *Can default options lead to credit card default?*, c. 2020** [CC].
  - Data: one bank's card customers (about one quarter of them). Method: empirical analysis of the invoice-default change.
  - Result: a small average effect, but "near-minimum and medium paying customers show an increase of 19.2 and 7.5 percentage points" in repayment.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY; repository not reachable), low-moderate confidence. — [NHH Brage](https://openaccess.nhh.no/nhh-xmlui/handle/11250/2734770)
- **SIFO, Poppe, Gyüre & Bilgrei, *Gjeldsregistrenes betydning 3.0. Rentebærende, usikret kreditt 2021–2025*, SIFO-rapport 3-2026; commissioned by Barne- og familiedepartementet** [UC]. OsloMet news 21 Apr 2026.
  - Method: register data plus industry informants.
  - Loan brokers (låneformidlere) use consent-based access to the debt registers to market refinancing and new loans. Such consents typically last 12 months.
  - Poppe: "Mange vet heller ikke hva et samtykke innebærer" (many don't know what a consent entails). Withdrawing consent requires logging in to the debt register, which "det er mange som ikke vet" (many don't know).
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate confidence. Lack of understanding is the researcher's assessment. — [OsloMet: Gjeldsregisteret hjelper bankene, men øker presset på låntakere](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/gjeldsregisteret-hjelper-bankene-men-oker-presset-pa-lantakere)

### Inferences
- INFERRED (moderate-high): a common Norwegian revolver mental model is "the card is safer and more controllable than a loan". This explains choosing card credit over cheaper instalment loans; flexibility is valued above price.
- INFERRED (moderate): minimum-payment behaviour across several cards is a hidden-risk state. It does not look like default, either to the consumer or in old survey measures, which supports a "quietly stretched multi-card holder" avatar.
- HYPOTHESIS: that Norwegians treat credit limits as "available money". This is plausible, but I found no Norwegian evidence that tests it.
- HYPOTHESIS: confusion between rewards and costs. No Norwegian evidence found.

### Gaps
- No Norwegian data located on treating the credit limit as one's own money, on multiple-card counts per person, or on how well people understand interest-free periods.
- The 2013 Finanstilsynet figures and the NHH thesis effects need checking against the originals.

## 4. Emotions, identity and norms: shame, control, stress, avoidance, confidence, debt norms, trust

### Takeaway
Debt for consumption carries a strong moral stigma in Norway: 57% call it shameful, and consumer loans have the lowest status of any loan type. Credit-card debt is *less* stigmatised than consumer loans despite being costlier. Norwegians are described as financially secure but impulsive spenders who are slow to cut back and seek help late. Control beliefs (locus of control) and self-efficacy matter more than knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- **SIFO 13-2019 (Poppe), summary 7 Feb 2020** [UC/CC].
  - "Hele 57 prosent oppgir at de synes lån til forbruk er skamfullt" (fully 57% say they find borrowing for consumption shameful).
  - Borrowing for consumption and pleasure "ble av informantene i undersøkelsen sett på som tegn på dårlig moral og mangel på selvdisiplin" (was seen by informants as a sign of poor morals and lack of self-discipline).
  - Loans secured on the home have the highest status. Consumer loans come "nederst på rangstigen" (bottom of the status ladder). "Kredittkortlån har høyere status, men er mye mer risikabelt i bruk" (card loans have higher status but are much riskier to use).
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate-high confidence. Sample size not shown. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)
- **SIFO, Kempson & Poppe, *Assessing the Levels of Financial Capability and Financial Well-being in Norway. A summary report*, SIFO Oppdragsrapport 19-2018** [GEN/CC]. OsloMet news 2 Jul 2019.
  - Method: international comparative survey. Data year and sample size not stated on page.
  - Segments: 57% financially secure; 34% doing well but with few reserves; 6% just getting by; about 3% struggling. Among those struggling, four in ten say paying bills on time is a constant fight.
  - Compared with the other countries, "bruker nordmenn penger mer impulsivt" (Norwegians spend money more impulsively), and easy credit reinforces this.
  - Named risk factors: "Ukontrollert kredittkortbruk, ingen sparing og det å ta opp lån for å betale regninger, mat og daglige utgifter" (uncontrolled credit-card use, no saving, borrowing for bills, food and daily expenses).
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate-high confidence. — [OsloMet: Økonomisk trygge nordmenn bruker mye penger](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/okonomisk-trygge-nordmenn-bruker-mye-penger)
- **Same page: inertia and late help-seeking** [GEN].
  - Earlier SIFO studies show "en treghet, kanskje også motvilje" (inertia, perhaps reluctance) to tighten spending even in difficulty.
  - "De fleste som oppsøker den økonomiske rådgivningstjenesten i Nav skulle ha kommet for lenge siden" (most who come to NAV's financial advice service should have come long ago).
  - Status: VERIFIED (researcher statement on institutional page), moderate confidence. This is expert judgement, not measured prevalence.
- **Birkeland & Vogt, "Youth Debt in a Life-Course Context: Young People's Ways into and out of Debt in Norway", *Nordic Welfare Research* 9(3), 2024, pp. 238–251, DOI 10.18261/nwr.9.3.3** [UC/GEN].
  - Method: biographical interviews with young debtors aged 25–32, plus expert interviews with welfare-state debt advisers.
  - Abstract: youth debt "appears to be related to classed shame associated with financial mismanagement, and to normative and gendered milestones in transitions to adulthood". Ways into and out of debt are "contextualized biographical processes".
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY abstract via DOAJ; idunn.no returned 403), moderate confidence for the abstract claims. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18261/nwr.9.3.3); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/f45c517363934854b164766003051ad7)
- **Kempson & Poppe, "The low self-efficacy trap: why people with vulnerabilities experience prolonged periods with payment problems", *Frontiers in Behavioral Economics*, 26 Apr 2024, DOI 10.3389/frbhe.2024.1368877** [UC/GEN].
  - Method: in-depth interviews with 28 people who had both payment difficulties and ill-health vulnerability, analysed with Bandura's social cognitive theory.
  - Finding: inflexible, bureaucratic routines at creditors, debt collectors and advisers erode debtors' self-efficacy and prolong problems.
  - Status: INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), moderate confidence. Norwegian setting inferred from the SIFO-led WellDebt project, whose page I opened and which says mechanisms "will be identified through qualitative interviews". — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frbhe.2024.1368877/full); [OsloMet WellDebt](https://www.oslomet.no/en/research/research-projects/welldebt)
- **SIFO 7-2024 (Gyüre & Lynum)** [GEN].
  - Young people get finance knowledge from social media, friends, websites and TV shows such as *Luksusfellen* (a debt-makeover reality show). One in ten has no one to turn to.
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate confidence. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/unge-fra-familier-med-darlig-rad-lerer-lite-om-privatokonomi-hjemme)
- **Trust and advice**
  - Bakkeli (2020) calls for "en nøytral nettportal med økonomisk rådgivning uavhengig av markedsinteresser og banker" (a neutral advice portal independent of market interests and banks). Bank/advisor ranks 4th of eight learning channels.
  - Status: VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate confidence for the ranking. The implied distrust of bank-led advice is the researcher's normative view, not a measured trust score. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/slik-lerer-vi-om-personlig-okonomi)

### Inferences
- INFERRED (moderate-high): status and shame drive product choice. Card debt is a socially "acceptable" way to borrow for consumption, so card debt may be easier to hide from oneself and others than a consumer loan. Avatars should reflect moral framing (self-discipline, "dårlig moral") rather than ignorance alone.
- INFERRED (moderate): class and gender shape youth debt shame (Birkeland & Vogt). Avoid one-size "irresponsible young borrower" stereotypes.
- INFERRED (moderate): low self-efficacy and control beliefs, not knowledge deficits, keep people stuck in payment problems (Kempson & Poppe 2017/2024).
- HYPOTHESIS: confident full-payers (transactors) take pride in "kontroll" (control) and see the card as a tool. Not directly measured in Norway.

### Gaps
- No Norwegian quantitative measure of stress, avoidance or confidence specific to credit cards was found.
- No measured trust-in-banks data was accessed: Norsk kundebarometer and Finans Norge surveys were not found on allowlisted pages.

## 6. Forbrukerrådet surveys and reports on credit cards, consumer loans, aggressive marketing and young people

### Takeaway
Forbrukerrådet's relevant outputs include:
- the 2015 10th-grader credit-card survey;
- annual *Bytteundersøkelsen* surveys (2026 edition covers 2025 credit uptake);
- a 2025/2026 report based on NAV debt-adviser interviews, recommending that a ban on active credit-card and consumer-loan marketing be considered.

All sit on storage02.forbrukerradet.no, so this section is snippet-level only.

### Cited Findings
- 2015 10th-grader survey: one in three do not realise card use is a loan; one in ten know the real interest level. INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), low-moderate. — [PDF](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2015/10/20150908-Rapport_10_klassinger_kredittkort.pdf)
- *Bytteundersøkelsen 2026*: 20% set up new forbrukslån, billån, kredittkort or "utsatt betaling" agreements in 2025. INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), low-moderate. — [PDF](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)
- Gjeldsrådgivning report: 10 in-depth interviews with NAV debt advisers. Recommends considering "et forbud mot aktiv markedsføring av kredittkort og forbrukslån", better information on loan costs and better credit assessments. INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), moderate for its existence and recommendations. — [PDF](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/rapport-gjeldsradgivning-2025.pdf)
- Forbrukerrådet, via Altinget opinion piece: about 1.1 million Norwegians have interest-bearing consumer debt. Forbrukerrådet wants to ban surveillance-based marketing and credit marketing on social media. INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY; advocacy source), low-moderate. — [Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/artikkel/tre-grep-for-aa-bremse-gjeldsspiraler)

Broker marketing based on debt-register consent is a new channel. VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/gjeldsregisteret-hjelper-bankene-men-oker-presset-pa-lantakere)

## 7. First-time versus experienced holders; full-balance payers versus revolvers

### Takeaway
There is no direct Norwegian study comparing first-time and experienced credit-card holders. On full payers versus revolvers:
- Older Finanstilsynet data (2013, snippet-only) suggest roughly half pay in full and about one in six pay only the minimum.
- Register data show about one-fifth of consumer-debt volume is non-interest-bearing, mostly card balances not yet due.
- Minimum-only payers responded most to the full-balance invoice default (NHH thesis, snippet-only).

### Cited Findings
- Finanstilsynet April 2025: 80.4% of total consumer debt in Gjeldsregisteret was interest-bearing at end of March 2025. Non-interest-bearing debt is mainly card debt not yet due.
  - Limitation: interest-bearing includes instalment loans, so the share of card *holders* who revolve cannot be derived from this.
  - Ages 18–29, 30–39 and 40–49 have a larger share of debt-collection cases than of consumer debt.
  - VERIFIED (primary), high confidence for the figures. — [Finanstilsynet PDF](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/4990ef/contentassets/aaf5f43e56c8450b97ba4a50190679eb/rapport-om-utviklingen-i-forbruksgjeld-april-2025.pdf)
- Finanstilsynet 2013 survey: about 46% paid in full or more, about 26% paid above the minimum but below the full balance, and about 17% paid only the minimum. INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), low-moderate. Old pre-2016 data.
- NHH thesis (c. 2020): after the full-balance invoice default, near-minimum payers increased repayment by 19.2 percentage points and medium payers by 7.5 percentage points. INFERRED (SNIPPET-ONLY), low-moderate. — [NHH Brage](https://openaccess.nhh.no/nhh-xmlui/handle/11250/2734770)
- SIFO 3-2026 (register data): about 25% of adults held interest-bearing unsecured credit in 2025. Only 16% had about the same unsecured debt in 2025 as in 2023; "nesten halvparten har økt opptak" (almost half increased borrowing).
  - In 2025, 171,407 people (4%) were under utleggstrekk (wage garnishment). 69% of them had interest-bearing credit. The probability of garnishment reaches 37.7% when interest-bearing credit exceeds NOK 500,000, and having such debt makes garnishment ten times as likely.
  - VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), high confidence (register-based). — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/gjeldsregisteret-hjelper-bankene-men-oker-presset-pa-lantakere)
- SIFO 13-2019: 32% of adults aged 18–80 had some unsecured debt (overdraft, consumer loan or card debt) in 2019. VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate-high. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)
- SIFO 7-2024: among 18–20-year-olds, "det er veldig få som har gjeld" (very few have debt) and they are "flinke til å spare og unngå lån" (good at saving and avoiding loans). VERIFIED (secondary-institutional), moderate. — [OsloMet](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/unge-fra-familier-med-darlig-rad-lerer-lite-om-privatokonomi-hjemme)

### Inferences
- INFERRED (moderate): first-time holders around 18–20 are mostly debt-free savers with mid-level knowledge and little school-based understanding. Risk appears later: 18–39-year-olds are over-represented in debt collection relative to their share of consumer debt.
- INFERRED (moderate): revolving debt is dynamic, not a fixed trait. Most people's unsecured balances changed between 2023 and 2025, and nearly half increased. "Revolver" avatars should be framed as states that people move into and out of.
- HYPOTHESIS: the full-balance invoice default (since 2016) has made full payment the passive path, so minimum payment is now an active choice. That could make today's minimum payers more deliberate, or more pressured, than pre-2016 cohorts. Not tested.

### Gaps
- No post-2016 Norwegian figure on the share of card holders who pay in full versus revolve.
- No Norwegian research comparing first-time and experienced holders.
- No data on number of cards per person or on credit-limit utilisation psychology.
