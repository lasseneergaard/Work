# Norwegians Pay by Card, Borrow Less, Rarely Compare

The Norwegian who researches credit cards is, on the evidence available, mostly a **payer rather than a borrower**. In 2025 **nearly 48% of Norwegians over 15 had a card balance that was not yet due, against 19% with an interest-bearing card balance** ([Norges Bank 2026](https://www.norges-bank.no/bankplassen/arkiv/2026/dyrtid-og-hoye-renter-har-ikke-fort-til-storre-forbrukslan/)). The interest-bearing share of all card balances fell from **about 74% in December 2019 to about 53% in November 2026** ([Gjeldsregisteret](https://www.gjeldsregisteret.com/); [Nøkkeltall](https://www.gjeldsregisteret.com/nokkeltall)). People open new cards for practical, transactional reasons. "Fordi det er praktisk" was the top stated reason in 2025 (48%), and travel and shop offers dominated in the two years before. Few shop around: **only 15% of card users searched the market in 2025, 8–11% moved a card in a year, and 26% of adults know Finansportalen** ([Forbrukerrådet, Bytteundersøkelsen 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). A smaller revolving group, typically middle-aged with balances around NOK 10,000, sees card credit as safer and more controllable than a consumer loan, even though it usually costs more. First-time researchers, the least-evidenced group, face eligibility rules and low product knowledge. For Enkellån, the persuasive message and the compliant message largely coincide: show the cost in kroner, say plainly that comparing is not applying, explain that the whole credit limit counts when a bank assesses a mortgage, and disclose that commission shapes the order of the list. Enkellån must also be honest about verified gaps in its product: it shows no currency surcharge, cash-withdrawal fee or minimum payment; its commission-influenced order puts the cheapest headline rate fifth; and signing up requires marketing consent. The five avatars below are evidence-informed composites, not measured segments. Meta's Financial products and services category removes most targeting in Norway, so the creative has to select the audience. A possible ban on credit marketing on social media makes primary validation and channel diversification urgent.

*Labels used throughout. **VERIFIED**: read in the original source or in the publishing institution's own summary of it. **INFERRED**: reasoned from verified evidence, or seen only in a search snippet. **HYPOTHESIS**: plausible but untested in Norway. Confidence is given as High, Moderate or Low. Access flags: **[full text]**, **[institutional summary]**, **[snippet-only]**, **[abstract-level]**. Tiers are defined in Section 2. Every Norwegian hook in this report is **proposed messaging**, not a quotation.*

## 1. Executive summary: payers outnumber borrowers, and comparison is an occasional, event-driven act

**Using a card to pay and using it to borrow are diverging in Norway.** At the national level the series move in opposite directions. Interest-bearing card balances fell about 27% in nominal terms between December 2019 and November 2026, like for like. Balances not yet due rose about 80% over the same period (researcher calculation on [Gjeldsregisteret](https://www.gjeldsregisteret.com/nokkeltall) register figures). In 2025, cards with a credit function carried **13.5% of Norwegian card transactions and about 21% of card payment value**. Their average payment was **NOK 633**, against NOK 365 for BankAxept (researcher calculation on [Norges Bank, Kunderetta betalingsformidling 2025](https://www.norges-bank.no/contentassets/37aa558585244e9cb45aea0d062fc52d/betalingsformidling_2025.pdf)). Revolving is concentrated rather than typical. It is most common among 45–54-year-olds (29% with an interest-bearing card balance), and the median interest-bearing balance is **"stabilt rundt 10 000 kroner i alle aldersgrupper"** (stable at around NOK 10,000 in all age groups) ([Norges Bank 2026](https://www.norges-bank.no/bankplassen/arkiv/2026/dyrtid-og-hoye-renter-har-ikke-fort-til-storre-forbrukslan/)). [VERIFIED, Tier 1, full text, High]

**Comparing cards is rare, and it is usually set off by an event rather than by a routine review.** Forbrukerrådet's switching survey (Ipsos online panel, about 2,000 adults a year) records the following ([Bytteundersøkelsen 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)):

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Card users who searched the market in 2025 | 15% |
| Card or store-account holders who moved or renegotiated, 2023 / 2024 / 2025 | 9% / 13% / 9% |
| Of those, renegotiated with their existing bank, each year | 2% |
| Bank-service switchers who simply contacted their own bank (2025) | 55% |
| Bank-service switchers who used Finansportalen (2025) | 15% |

The reasons people give for opening a new card are mostly about convenience. In the 2025 data the top answer was "praktisk" at 48%. In earlier years, "gode vilkår ved kjøp av reise, tilbud i butikker" (good terms on travel purchases and shop offers) led, with 56% and 59%. Liquidity motives were a minority: 12% said they had no money in their account when they wanted to buy something, and 6–19% cited unforeseen expenses ([Bytteundersøkelsen 2024](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2024/06/rapport-bytteundersokelsen-2024.pdf); [2025](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2025/05/bytteundersokelsen-2025.pdf); [2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). [VERIFIED, Tier 1, full text, High for figures; the answer lists changed between years]

**Norwegian psychology around card credit is moral as much as financial.** SIFO's 2019 survey, read through OsloMet's summary, reports three findings ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)). [VERIFIED, institutional summary, Moderate–High]

- **57% find borrowing for consumption shameful.**
- Consumer loans sit at the bottom of the status ladder, while card loans "har høyere status, men er mye mer risikabelt i bruk" (have higher status but are much riskier to use).
- Many believe consumer loans are both dearer and less controllable than credit cards. This "forbrukslån-paradoks" leads people to choose costlier card credit.

The only first-person Norwegian consumer words about cards in the evidence are short survey answers. They are practical and specific: "Trygg netthandel", "Mulighet til Apple pay", "Det gamle kredittkortet mistet sin bonusordning, som var svært gunstig". Testimony from NAV debt advisers describes the darker edge of the market: cards maxed out after divorce or illness, a student who called his credit account his "bufferkonto", letterbox anxiety, and years of secrecy ([Forbrukerrådet, NAV-adviser report](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/rapport-gjeldsradgivning-2025.pdf)). [VERIFIED, full text; adviser voices, not consumer voices]

**Enkellån's verified proposition is narrower than its headline.** The site lists 8 card and broker offers, ordered by Enkellån with an order that is "påvirkes av avtalene vi har med partnerne og av provisjonen vi mottar" (influenced by partner agreements and commission). Viewing the list is free and needs no sign-up. Signing up requires mandatory consent to marketing by phone, email, WhatsApp and SMS, and to social-media targeting. The step-2 questionnaire hides only cards whose age or income requirements the user does not meet. The cheapest headline effective rate, Unofinans at 23.10%, is listed fifth, and the site does not show currency surcharge, cash-withdrawal fees or minimum payment. All of these are fields the public comparator Finansportalen treats as decision-relevant ([Enkellån /kredittkort](https://enkellaan.no/kredittkort); [/kredittkort/tilbud](https://enkellaan.no/kredittkort/tilbud); [Finansportalen](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/finansportalen/bank/kredittkort)). [VERIFIED, full text, High]

**Regulation sets most of the creative brief.** Under [finansavtaleforskriften](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2022-09-19-1612) §§ 3-3 to 3-5, three rules apply to credit advertising:

- **Every credit ad must carry a representative example.**
- **Benefits may not be more prominent than the cost information.**
- **Speed, ease or a low threshold may not be emphasised, and no interest rate may be shown more prominently than the effective rate.**

Forbrukertilsynet's 2021 letter to credit actors adds that the company behind a secondary brand must appear in the Facebook ad itself, that affiliates should avoid "søk om lån her" (apply for a loan here), and that lead forms must state they are not applications ([Forbrukertilsynet 2021](https://forbrukertilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/orienteringsbrev-til-kredittaktorer-vedrorende-bruk-av-sekundaere-merkenavn-markedsforing-av-k.pdf)). Meta lists Norway among the countries where credit-card ads, including brand ads, must run in the Financial products and services special ad category ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/567423788405762)). In that category, gender selection, detailed-targeting exclusions and most interest targeting are removed. [VERIFIED, High]

## 2. Methodology and evidence quality: a structured evidence review with significant access gaps

### What kind of review this is

This is a **structured evidence review, not a systematic review**. Seven thematic research passes on 18 November 2026 gathered and read sources against a shared brief:

- market data;
- consumer psychology;
- qualitative language and Forbrukerrådet surveys;
- Enkellån's offering and the comparison landscape;
- regulation and Meta policy;
- comparison journeys;
- behavioural mechanisms.

There was no registered protocol, no exhaustive database search, no dual screening and no formal risk-of-bias scoring. The notes record searching in both Norwegian and English (for example "kredittkort", "forbruksgjeld", "rammekreditt", "Bytteundersøkelsen", "present bias credit card") across institutional, academic, regulatory and commercial sites, but they do not contain a full search log. Evidence is therefore graded by source type and access status, as set out below, rather than by a formal quality instrument.

### Inclusion and exclusion criteria

**Included:**

- Norwegian data from 2019–2026 on card ownership, use, revolving debt, comparison or switching, and consumer attitudes. Older Norwegian data was kept only where nothing newer exists, and is labelled with its year: Finanstilsynet's invoicing survey from before 2013, Forbrukerrådet's 2015 survey of tenth-graders, and a 2014 survey on online payment.
- Norwegian law and regulatory guidance in force in November 2026.
- Meta's first-party policy pages.
- International and Nordic peer-reviewed papers, working papers and regulatory studies on behavioural mechanisms.
- Enkellån's own pages, read in their raw HTML.
- Commercial or commissioned market research, where the sponsor could be identified.

**Excluded:**

- Claims that could not be traced to a URL. Examples are an "Opinion for Lendo, March 2026" survey, a Nordea claim about TikTok use, and a Svea figure on in-store card use that had no source link.
- Search-volume data, which was excluded by design.
- The earlier Danish avatar set, which was used only for the mapping in Appendix A.
- Enkellån's consumer-loan homepage claims, which do not apply to the card product.

## 3. Norwegian market and consumer context: more card payments, fewer card loans, tighter rules

### Card spending has grown while card borrowing has fallen

The national register shows a structural shift. On 18 November 2026, total card limits were **NOK 288.4 bn**. Of that, **NOK 79.4 bn** was in use: **NOK 42.3 bn interest-bearing and NOK 37.0 bn not yet due** ([Gjeldsregisteret](https://www.gjeldsregisteret.com/)). That puts about 53% of card balances in the interest-bearing category, against about 74% at the end of 2019. Year by year, the interest-bearing share fell from 74.3% (2019) to 74.1%, 70.5% and 66.6% (2022), and stood at 54.2% in November 2026 on the unadjusted basis (researcher calculation on [Nøkkeltall](https://www.gjeldsregisteret.com/nokkeltall)). About **72.5% of all granted limits are unused**. Finanstilsynet describes non-interest-bearing debt as "i hovedsak av kredittkortgjeld som ikke har forfalt" (mainly credit card debt not yet due), and it grew 13.6% in the year to March 2025 ([Finanstilsynet 2025](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/4990ef/contentassets/aaf5f43e56c8450b97ba4a50190679eb/rapport-om-utviklingen-i-forbruksgjeld-april-2025.pdf)). Norges Bank confirms the pattern at the level of individuals. The share of people over 15 with a balance not yet due rose from 37% (2022) to "nesten 48 prosent" (nearly 48%) in 2025, "særlig stor blant 25–34-åringer" (especially among 25–34-year-olds). The share with interest-bearing card debt fell from 21% to 19%. Norges Bank links part of the rise to DNB and S-banken enabling Apple Pay on their credit cards in December 2024 ([Norges Bank 2026](https://www.norges-bank.no/bankplassen/arkiv/2026/dyrtid-og-hoye-renter-har-ikke-fort-til-storre-forbrukslan/)). [VERIFIED, Tier 1, High]

| Year-end (unadjusted) | Card limits (NOK bn) | Interest-bearing (NOK bn) | Not yet due (NOK bn) | Interest-bearing share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dec 2019 | 263.7 | 59.9 | 20.7 | 74.3% |
| Dec 2020 | 244.3 | 54.2 | 18.9 | 74.1% |
| Dec 2021 | 231.8 | 49.6 | 20.8 | 70.5% |
| Dec 2022 | 231.9 | 46.8 | 23.5 | 66.6% |
| 18 Nov 2026 | 291.1 | 44.0 | 37.2 | 54.2% |

The register covers "rammekreditt", which includes some non-bank revolving products such as Klarna-type instalment credit ([SSB 2026](https://www.ssb.no/inntekt-og-forbruk/inntekt-og-formue/artikler/4-av-10-har-rentebaerende-forbruksgjeld)).*

### Cards are a minority payment tool, used for larger tickets

Norway runs on debit. BankAxept handled 46% of Norwegian card transactions in 2025 and 70% at physical terminals. **Norwegian-issued international credit cards numbered 4.459 million at end-2025**, down every year from 4.861 million in 2022. Over the same period, credit-function transactions rose from 323.9 million (2022) to 449.2 million (2025), and spending rose from NOK 202.1 bn to NOK 285.4 bn ([Norges Bank 2025](https://www.norges-bank.no/contentassets/37aa558585244e9cb45aea0d062fc52d/betalingsformidling_2025.pdf)). [VERIFIED, High] Fewer cards carrying more spending is **consistent with consolidation into fewer cards per holder** [INFERRED, Moderate; no source states it]. The higher average credit-card ticket (NOK 633, against NOK 365–385 for debit) suggests cards are used for larger purchases, but Norway publishes no split by purchase category [INFERRED, Low–Moderate]. Norges Bank's figures on online, foreign and mobile payment are not split between credit and debit.

### Regulation since 2019 reshaped borrowing and the meaning of a credit limit

Four rules shape what a card means to a Norwegian consumer. All are [VERIFIED, legal text, High].

- **Affordability counts the full limit.** Under utlånsforskriften § 5, a bank must assess affordability "for rammekreditter … full utnyttelse av samlede rammer" (as if total limits were fully used), with a stress test of a 3-point rise and a minimum assessment rate of 7%.
- **Small card portfolios are exempt.** Under § 3, cards are exempt only while total card limits stay at or below **NOK 30,000**.
- **Minimum payments must clear the debt in five years.** Under § 13, the minimum payment must repay used credit within 5 years as a serial loan ([Lovdata utlånsforskriften](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2020-12-09-2648)).
- **Limit increases need a full credit check.** Finansavtaleloven § 5-2 requires a thorough creditworthiness assessment before any "vesentlig økning" (significant increase) of a limit, and § 5-4 obliges refusal where repayment is unlikely ([Lovdata finansavtaleloven](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2020-12-18-146)).

Since 2016, card invoices must show the **total outstanding balance in the amount field**, with the minimum shown as an option and a table of the costs of different repayment choices ([Finanstilsynet Rundskriv 10/2016](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/contentassets/6ee34941605a4fdea9566bd9b5ecd330/rundskriv10_2016_fakturering_av_kredittkort.pdf)).

Official commentary credits these changes with falling consumer debt. Norges Bank writes: "Etter at forbruksgjeld ble inkludert i utlånsreguleringen i 2019 falt omfanget av denne gjelden i flere år" (after consumer debt was included in lending regulation in 2019, its volume fell for several years). Industry informants told SIFO that "de useriøse tilbyderne falt fra" (the unscrupulous providers dropped out) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2026](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/gjeldsregisteret-hjelper-bankene-men-oker-presset-pa-lantakere)). About **95,000 people converted card credit to instalment loans during 2023** ([Norges Bank 2024](https://www.norges-bank.no/bankplassen/arkiv/2024/ny-vekst-i-forbruksgjelden/)). [VERIFIED]

### Prices: high rates, near-zero average fees

In the register, the median nominal rate on "rammekreditt" was **19.80% in December 2019 and 20.49% in December 2022** ([Nøkkeltall](https://www.gjeldsregisteret.com/nokkeltall)). Forbrukerrådet states that "Flertallet av de effektive kredittkortrentene i Finansportalen lå mellom 27 og 28 prosent i januar 2026" (most effective card rates on Finansportalen were 27–28% in January 2026), and that the portal listed **182 credit card providers** on 12 January 2026 ([Forbrukerrådet innspill, Jan 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/01/20260114-forbrukerradets-innspill-til-finansmarkedsmeldingen-endelig.pdf)). That document is an advocacy submission. Annual fees, by contrast, are negligible on average. On 1 January 2026 the weighted average fee for an international credit card was **NOK 23.10 for non-programme customers and NOK 4.20 for programme customers** ([Norges Bank 2025](https://www.norges-bank.no/contentassets/37aa558585244e9cb45aea0d062fc52d/betalingsformidling_2025.pdf)). [VERIFIED] With fees this low, cards plausibly compete on interest-free days, benefits and limits rather than on fees [INFERRED, Moderate]. Consumer-loan banks are the largest holders of revolving credit ([Norges Bank 2024](https://www.norges-bank.no/bankplassen/arkiv/2024/ny-vekst-i-forbruksgjelden/)). Credit-management firms held about 24% of interest-bearing revolving credit at end-2025, up from 13% in 2020 ([Norges Bank 2026](https://www.norges-bank.no/bankplassen/arkiv/2026/dyrtid-og-hoye-renter-har-ikke-fort-til-storre-forbrukslan/)). No public source gives issuer market shares.

### A distressed tail, not the typical cardholder

Burdensome unsecured debt, defined as at least 25% of gross household income, affected **5.1% of people (278,700) in 2024, down from 7.1% in 2020**. It is concentrated among the following groups ([SSB 2026](https://www.ssb.no/inntekt-og-forbruk/inntekt-og-formue/artikler/4-av-10-har-rentebaerende-forbruksgjeld)):

- men living alone (8.3%);
- non-home-owners with low financial wealth (17.79%);
- households whose main earner is an immigrant (7.8%).

In burdened households, cards make up about 30% of the debt and consumer loans about 70%. At 30 June 2026, **280,851 credit card collection cases totalled NOK 19.5 bn, of which 44.6% was accrued interest** ([Finanstilsynet inkasso H1 2026](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/analyser-og-statistikk/statistikk-for-inkasso/2026/html/inkassostatistikk-for-forste-halvar-2026/)). SIFO's register analysis finds that holding interest-bearing unsecured debt is associated with a **ten times higher probability of wage garnishment** (utleggstrekk) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2026](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/gjeldsregisteret-hjelper-bankene-men-oker-presset-pa-lantakere)). [VERIFIED; associations, not causes] For advertising, this tail matters for the reason given in markedsføringsloven § 6(3): the fairness of a practice is judged from the perspective of a vulnerable group it reaches.

### Buy-now-pay-later blurs the line between paying and borrowing

In 2025, **29% of households used "utsatt betaling"** (BNPL), and **11% opened a new BNPL agreement**, compared with 7% who opened a card or store account ([Bytteundersøkelsen 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). Forbrukerrådet reports that "mange ikke tar inn over seg at bruk av BNPL-løsninger, slik som Klarna, tilsvarer å bruke kredittkort" (many don't take on board that using BNPL such as Klarna is equivalent to using a credit card) ([Forbrukerrådet årsrapport 2025](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/04/fr-aarsrapport-2025.pdf)). NAV advisers report "Klarna-gjeld blir stadig mer vanlig" (Klarna debt is becoming ever more common) ([NAV-adviser report, p.15](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/rapport-gjeldsradgivning-2025.pdf)). [VERIFIED]

Enkellån's genuine strengths are verified ones:

## 4. First-time researchers and existing cardholders: one group is well measured, the other mostly inferred

### Existing cardholders choose incidentally and rarely revisit

The evidence on existing holders is relatively rich. Between 61% and 76% of households report holding a credit card or store account, depending on how the question was worded. In any single year only **7–9% open a new card or store account**, **8–11% move a card** and **2% renegotiate one** ([Bytteundersøkelsen 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). Finanstilsynet observes that a bank "switch" often means opening one product elsewhere, "(f.eks. kredittkort)" (for example a credit card), without ending the existing relationship, and calls this "rasjonell forbrukeradferd" (rational consumer behaviour) ([Finanstilsynet 2024](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/494e0e/contentassets/57018beddcf64384ba0df63e5e00f992/finanstilsynets-rapport-om-konkurransen-i-det-norske-bankmarkedet.pdf)). [VERIFIED, High] Switching cards is therefore usually **additive**: people add a card, and may keep the old one. The open-ended answers show how often acquisition is incidental, for example "Byttet bank for å få boliglån, og de anbefalte å opprette kredittkort i samme slengen" (switched bank to get a mortgage, and they recommended opening a credit card at the same time), "Kredittkort koblet opp med ny jobb" (credit card linked to a new job) and "Coop byttet leverandør" (Coop changed provider). Inertia is driven mainly by satisfaction and by small perceived gains. Among people who did not switch any bank service in 2025, 48% said "er fornøyd/ har gode betingelser" (I'm satisfied / have good terms) and 26% said "tror ikke det er så mye å spare" (I don't think there's much to save), while 12% said switching is "for krevende og komplisert" (too demanding and complicated).

### First-time researchers are an evidence gap

There is **no Norwegian data on age at first credit card, the share of 18–29-year-olds holding a card, or first-time compared with experienced holders** (gaps recorded in the market notes). What exists is adjacent:

- **Youth knowledge.** SIFO finds 18–20-year-olds have knowledge "midt på den skalaen" (mid-scale), know most about saving and "beregning av renter" (calculating interest), and that "det er veldig få som har gjeld" (very few have debt). Two in three say they were not taught personal finance at school, and one in ten has no one to ask for help ([OsloMet/SIFO 2024](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/unge-fra-familier-med-darlig-rad-lerer-lite-om-privatokonomi-hjemme); [OsloMet/SIFO 2025](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/norske-ungdommer-har-middels-kunnskap-om-privatokonomi)). [VERIFIED, institutional summary, Moderate]
- **Awareness of the neutral comparator.** Only **12% of 18–24-year-olds are aware of Finansportalen**, against 31% of 30–44-year-olds ([Bytteundersøkelsen 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). [VERIFIED, High]
- **Older evidence from minors.** Forbrukerrådet's 2015 Norstat survey of 300 tenth-graders found that 27% said paying by credit card is not taking a loan, only 11% knew typical rates exceed 20%, and **49% expected to get a card at 18** ([Forbrukerrådet 2015](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2015/10/20150908-Rapport_10_klassinger_kredittkort.pdf)). [VERIFIED, but 2015 data from 14–15-year-olds, Low relevance today]
- **Eligibility rules.** Enkellån's FAQ says most issuers require age 18, "noen 20 eller 23 år" (some 20 or 23), plus steady income and no payment remarks ([Enkellån](https://enkellaan.no/kredittkort)). Finansportalen records minimum and maximum age and the minimum income for the lowest limit for every card ([Finansportalen](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/finansportalen/bank/kredittkort)). [VERIFIED]

Registered debt and collection cases tell the same story. Ages 18–29 hold only **3.5% of defaulted consumer debt**, yet they have a larger share of consumer-debt collection cases than of consumer debt ([Finanstilsynet inkasso H1 2026](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/analyser-og-statistikk/statistikk-for-inkasso/2026/html/inkassostatistikk-for-forste-halvar-2026/); [Finanstilsynet 2025](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/4990ef/contentassets/aaf5f43e56c8450b97ba4a50190679eb/rapport-om-utviklingen-i-forbruksgjeld-april-2025.pdf)). The pattern is small debts, but disproportionate trouble with them. [VERIFIED]

## 5. Spending, borrowing and repayment: four different outcomes that must not be conflated

Four distinct behaviours get blurred in Norwegian card discussion: holding a card or limit **[OWN]**, paying with it **[USE]**, borrowing on it with interest **[REV]**, and comparing or acquiring cards **[ACQ]**. Each Norwegian indicator measures only one of them, and several are routinely misread.

| Indicator | Measures | Does **not** measure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Three cautions follow from this separation. First, **heavy use is not heavy borrowing**. The 45–54 group has both the highest transactor share (nearly 60% with a balance not yet due) and the highest revolver share (29%), so age alone does not separate payers from borrowers. Second, **revolving is a state, not a trait**. Only 16% of adults with unsecured debt had about the same level in 2025 as in 2023, and nearly half had increased it ([OsloMet/SIFO 2026](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/gjeldsregisteret-hjelper-bankene-men-oker-presset-pa-lantakere)). Third, **no Norwegian dataset links comparison behaviour to repayment behaviour**. Nobody knows whether people who compare cards are disproportionately transactors or revolvers, which is the single most important unknown for Enkellån's targeting. Section 16 sets out how to measure it.

On purchase categories, the only Norwegian evidence is SIFO 2019. It found that cards are used "særlig til reiser (44 prosent), kjøp av forbruksvarer (50 prosent) og uforutsette utgifter (30 prosent)" (especially for travel, 44%; consumer goods, 50%; and unforeseen expenses, 30%) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)). The summary does not say whether the base is all holders or only those carrying debt. [VERIFIED, institutional summary, Moderate; it predates the 2021 regulation]

### Paraphrased synthesis (not quotes)

The following is the research team's synthesis across the sources' descriptions, not a quotation. People learn personal finance through costly trial and error. Debt carries a moral meaning tied to self-discipline. Life transitions and crises (divorce, illness, leaving home, rising housing costs) open the way into card debt. Avoidance shows up as letterbox anxiety and years of secrecy, and it is sustained by fear of judgement. Fine print and the blurred line between "paying by card" and "borrowing" hide the cost. No prevalence is claimed for any of these themes. One more item is deliberately excluded: an NTNU thesis whose title appears to quote an informant ("Jeg kan ikke noe særlig om økonomi jeg …", roughly "I don't really know much about finance, me …") was seen only as a truncated search title, so it is **not used as a quote**.

## 8. Behavioural psychology: what Norway actually shows, and what is borrowed from elsewhere

Only two mechanisms have direct Norwegian empirical support: **debt-type mental accounting with stigma**, and (weakly) **decoupling and present bias as predictors of delinquency**. Disclosure simplification has close Danish experimental support. Everything else is inferred from UK and US studies read at abstract level, or remains a hypothesis. International effect sizes come from markets with higher revolving rates and looser rules, so they are **upper bounds for Norway**. Each mechanism below is described by its definition, the evidence, the Norwegian link, the implication for Enkellån, and the uncertainty around it.

### Loss aversion and cost framing

**Definition.** Losses weigh more heavily than equal gains, and equivalent information framed differently changes choices. **Evidence.** No credit-specific loss-versus-gain framing experiment was found. The nearest evidence concerns *currency-amount* framing. In a US field RCT with 1,441 payday borrowers, showing the accumulated dollar cost of fees cut borrowing by **23%**, against 16% for APR information ([Bertrand & Morse, J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/information-disclosure-cognitive-biases-and-payday-borrowing-united-states)). The FCA found in 2026 that adding the total amount repayable to APR improves comparison ([FCA 2026](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/research-notes/research-note-navigating-disclosure-trade-balancing-flexibility-and-standardisation-cost-credit)). [Tier 3, abstract-level] **Norwegian link.** Forbrukerrådet itself communicates in cost-in-kroner and loss-like terms: "nesten tre ganger så mye tilbake som vi lånte" (nearly three times as much back as we borrowed), and card debt at 25% "som dobler seg hvert tredje år" (that doubles every three years) ([Årsrapport 2025](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/04/fr-aarsrapport-2025.pdf); [innspill Jan 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/01/20260114-forbrukerradets-innspill-til-finansmarkedsmeldingen-endelig.pdf)). **Implication.** Lead with kroner. Test "you pay NOK X" against "you avoid paying NOK X" rather than assuming either works better. Loss frames shown to financially stressed people risk breaching markedsføringsloven § 6(3) and § 9. **Uncertainty.** Applicability of loss framing to Norway is a HYPOTHESIS (Low–Moderate). Kroner framing is INFERRED (Moderate–High).

### Present bias

**Definition.** People overweight immediate costs and benefits, so they make plans they later fail to keep; "naive" agents don't foresee this in themselves. **Evidence.** Present-biased US consumers were **about 15 percentage points more likely to hold card debt and held about 25% larger balances** ([Meier & Sprenger 2010](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aejapp:v:2:y:2010:i:1:p:193-210)). Many cardholders fail to stick to paydown plans they set themselves ([Kuchler & Pagel 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:139:y:2021:i:2:p:359-388)). In Danske Bank data, Danish high-cost borrowers appear more present-biased than other consumers ([Hansen 2022](https://www.econ.ku.dk/cebi/publikationer/working-papers/CEBI_WP_07-22.rev.pdf)). [Tier 3 and Nordic, abstract-level] **Norwegian link.** An NHH PhD using one Norwegian bank's card data from 2008–2011 found that lower concern for future consequences raises the risk of delinquency ([Huse 2019](https://openaccess.nhh.no/nhh-xmlui/handle/11250/2640771)) [snippet-only]. An adviser describes the same pattern: "Kanskje de tenker å løse det neste måned og så gjør man ikke det da" (maybe they think they'll sort it next month, and then you don't) ([NAV-adviser report, p.24](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/rapport-gjeldsradgivning-2025.pdf)). **Implication.** This mechanism applies to the revolving minority, not the transactor majority. Never use immediacy copy such as "kortet i posten i morgen" (card in the post tomorrow); it would also breach § 3-5. Encourage people to judge a card by their past repayment behaviour, not their intentions. **Uncertainty.** INFERRED, Moderate–High for revolvers. No Norwegian study measures present bias directly. Some apparent impatience is really lack of liquidity ([Guttman-Kenney et al. 2025](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/130080)).

### Mental accounting and payment decoupling

**Definition.** People keep money in non-fungible mental "accounts". Deferred payment weakens the link between spending and its cost (decoupling). **Evidence.** The credit-card "cashless effect" is **small and weakening**: a 2024 meta-analysis covering about 71 studies found a small, declining, location-dependent effect ([Schomburgk et al. 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jouret/v100y2024i3p382-403.html)), and a 692-person replication found no effect ([Liu & Dewitte 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joreco/v60y2021ics0969698921000382.html)). [Tier 3] **Norwegian link.** This is the clearest Norwegian mechanism. SIFO's "forbrukslån-paradoks" (consumer-loan paradox) finds that many believe "forbrukslån både er dyrere og mindre kontrollerbart enn kredittkort" (both more expensive and less controllable than credit cards), so "velger mange heller et mye dyrere kredittkortlån" (many choose a much more expensive card loan instead) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)) [VERIFIED, institutional summary]. Register rates show the gap: median nominal **20.5% on revolving credit versus 12.9% on instalment loans** in December 2022 ([SSB 2024](https://www.ssb.no/inntekt-og-forbruk/inntekt-og-formue/artikler/folk-med-lavinntekt-tynges-oftere-av-forbruksgjeld)). Advisers report a student whose "bufferkonto" (buffer account) was his credit account, and BNPL not being perceived as credit. **Implication.** Separate the card's two jobs explicitly: a payment tool, which costs nothing if paid in full, and a loan, which is one of the dearest ways to borrow. Never call the interest-free period "free money". **Uncertainty.** Debt-type accounting is VERIFIED (Moderate). A credit-versus-debit spending premium in Norway is a HYPOTHESIS (Low), because Norway's near-cashless baseline differs from the literature's cash comparison.

### Choice overload

**Definition.** Large or complex choice sets lower confidence and raise deferral. **Evidence.** Meta-analyses find a mean effect **near zero** ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/can-there-ever-be-too-many-options-a-meta-analytic-review-of-choice-overload-scheibehenne-benjamin/10008756224)). The effect becomes real when choice sets are complex, tasks are difficult, preferences are uncertain and people want to minimise effort ([Chernev et al. 2015](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual)). In complex choices, consumers delegate to **rank position and reviews** ([European Commission comparison-tools study](https://commission.europa.eu/publications/comparison-tools-and-third-party-verification-schemes_en)). [Tier 3] **Norwegian link.** Finansportalen lists 182 providers and more than 15 fields per card. Enkellån shows 8. Card choice meets all four of Chernev's conditions, because people do not know whether they will revolve [INFERRED]. **Implication.** Enkellån's short list is a genuine simplicity advantage. But if users lean on rank position, a **commission-influenced order becomes the decision**. That is both an ethical exposure and a reputational one. Offer a "sort by effective rate" option. **Uncertainty.** HYPOTHESIS/INFERRED, Low–Moderate. No Norwegian test exists.

### Status quo bias and switching inertia

**Definition.** People stay with their current provider despite better alternatives, because of real or perceived switching costs, the effort of paying attention, or the absence of a trigger. **Evidence.** UK current-account switching runs at about 3% a year, with "a lack of trigger points" identified as a cause ([CMA retail banking](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a8c0fb40f0b608a7000002/summary-of-final-report-retail-banking-investigation.pdf)). Cardholders are very reluctant to switch, with an estimated switching cost of about $150 ([Shui & Ausubel](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/b3bc4c71-9c90-4dfe-9ddb-dbfd108a0296)). [Tier 3] **Norwegian link.** Card renegotiation runs at 2% a year. Non-switchers cite satisfaction (48%) and "ikke så mye å spare" (not much to save, 26%). Product bundles hamper comparison ([Bytte 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf); [Finanstilsynet 2026](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/contentassets/8765573deb55464dafb59acb2210b6ec/forbrukernes-situasjon-i-innskuddsmarkedet.pdf)). [VERIFIED, descriptive] **Implication.** Advertise at **trigger points**: a trip, a product closure, a bank switch, a mortgage. Do not run generic "switch and save" copy. Quantify in NOK only where Enkellån can document the figure. **Uncertainty.** Bank-level inertia is VERIFIED; card-specific inertia is INFERRED (Moderate).

### Overconfidence about future borrowing

**Definition.** People underestimate how often they will carry a balance, so they ignore the interest rate when choosing a card. **Evidence.** In a US mail experiment, consumers chose short, low teaser rates that turned out worse in hindsight ([Shui & Ausubel](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/b3bc4c71-9c90-4dfe-9ddb-dbfd108a0296)). Issuers design back-loaded pricing around such biases ([Ru & Schoar, NBER](https://www.nber.org/digest/sep16/w22360.html)). [Tier 3] **Norwegian link.** There is only indirect evidence. "Flere menn enn kvinner mener de er flinke med penger" (more men than women think they're good with money) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/slik-lerer-vi-om-personlig-okonomi)), while objective test scores show the reverse gender gap ([Nyhus 2023 via ScienceNorway](https://www.sciencenorway.no/economy-finance-gender-differences/women-with-higher-education-know-as-little-about-personal-finance-as-men-with-a-primary-school-education/2282113), snippet-only). Overconfidence is possible but **not demonstrated** in Norwegian data. **Implication.** Benefit-led copy should include a check on future borrowing, for example "if you sometimes don't pay it all…". Do not build personas on gender. **Uncertainty.** INFERRED, Moderate for the mechanism. Its prevalence in Norway is a HYPOTHESIS.

### Risk perception

**Definition.** Subjective judgement of how likely and how severe a harm is. It often diverges from objective risk. **Evidence (Norway).** Cards "oppleves som trygt" (are experienced as safe), while SIFO's lead researcher calls card debt "det skumleste" (the scariest) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)). Teenagers who rejected cards in 2015 mostly feared not being able to repay (29%) or debt collection and payment remarks (10%) ([Forbrukerrådet 2015](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2015/10/20150908-Rapport_10_klassinger_kredittkort.pdf)). Holding interest-bearing unsecured debt is associated with **ten times** the risk of wage garnishment ([OsloMet/SIFO 2026](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/gjeldsregisteret-hjelper-bankene-men-oker-presset-pa-lantakere)). Enkellån's copy is built around the risk of being credit-checked or rejected ("ikke en søknad", "ingen kredittsjekk"), which signals that the company believes these fears exist. That is company positioning, not consumer evidence. **Implication.** Use calm, absolute, Norwegian facts. Avoid fear appeals. Reassure about *comparison* risk ("comparing isn't applying") without implying that *credit* is easy to get (§ 3-5). **Uncertainty.** The perception gap is VERIFIED (Moderate). How strongly fear of credit checks drives comparison behaviour is a HYPOTHESIS.

### Decision avoidance

**Definition.** People postpone or avoid decisions that feel aversive, complex or threatening. **Evidence (Norway).** Advisers report "postkasseangst" (letterbox anxiety) and people who "tør ikke eller vet ikke hvordan de skal logge seg inn på Digipost og Altinn" (don't dare or don't know how to log in to Digipost and Altinn) (p.31). SIFO notes "en treghet, kanskje også motvilje" (an inertia, perhaps a reluctance) about tightening spending ([OsloMet/SIFO 2019](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/okonomisk-trygge-nordmenn-bruker-mye-penger)). In 2025, 14% of non-switchers said "har ikke kommet så langt ennå, men vurderer" (haven't got that far yet, but I'm considering it) ([Bytte 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). [VERIFIED] **Implication.** Give one low-commitment next step. Enkellån's verified option to "se kortene uten å melde deg på" (see the cards without signing up) is the right affordance, and ads should point to it. **Uncertainty.** The evidence comes mainly from distressed populations; its relevance to ordinary card comparers is INFERRED (Moderate).

### Trust and source credibility

**Definition.** How expert and trustworthy a source is perceived to be, which governs how far people rely on its rankings. **Evidence.** UK comparison-site users report high trust but distrust how their data are used, often don't realise the sites earn commission, and don't know how to complain ([CMA 2017](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/59c93546e5274a77468120d6/digital-comparison-tools-market-study-final-report.pdf)). Across the EU, 65% of comparison-tool users had experienced a problem ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/publications/comparison-tools-and-third-party-verification-schemes_en)). [Tier 3] **Norwegian link.** There is **no Norwegian survey of trust in commercial comparison sites**. SIFO calls for "en nøytral nettportal … uavhengig av markedsinteresser og banker" (a neutral portal independent of market interests and banks) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/slik-lerer-vi-om-personlig-okonomi)). An adviser's suspicion, "kanskje har de provisjon? … det er en kynisk bransje" (maybe they get commission? … it's a cynical industry) (p.51), shows the frame a sceptical audience may bring. **Implication.** Disclosure should be a selling point, not small print: who runs the site (Drive Media ApS), how it is paid, how the order is set, and that users can view the list without signing up. Never claim to be "uavhengig" (independent) or the "beste" (best). **Uncertainty.** The pattern is INFERRED (Low–Moderate); Norwegian trust levels are a HYPOTHESIS.

### Financial self-efficacy

**Definition.** A person's belief in their ability to manage financial tasks. **Evidence (Norway).** SIFO's financial-wellbeing model found that locus of control mattered at every level while "knowledge and experience had little or no effect" ([Kempson, Finney & Poppe 2017, via Bristol PFRC](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/geography/research/pfrc/themes/capability-behaviours-wellbeing/financial-wellbeing-conceptual-model/), snippet-only). A 2024 interview study (n=28) describes a "low self-efficacy trap" in which rigid creditor routines prolong payment problems ([Kempson & Poppe 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frbhe.2024.1368877/full), snippet-only). Advisers describe "«lille meg mot en kreditor»" ("little me against a creditor") (p.31). Self-rated knowledge of "ta opp forbrukslån/kredittkortlån" (taking out consumer loans or card loans) ranks 6th of 7 personal-finance domains ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/slik-lerer-vi-om-personlig-okonomi)). **Implication.** Build self-efficacy with plain-language explanations and a single clear action ("check these three numbers"), not with knowledge dumps. **Uncertainty.** INFERRED, Moderate. The underlying sources are summaries or snippets.

### Social norms, shame and status

**Definition.** Perceived social approval of a behaviour, and the self-conscious emotion of shame when one falls short of it. **Evidence (Norway).** **57% find borrowing for consumption shameful.** Borrowing for pleasure is seen as "tegn på dårlig moral og mangel på selvdisiplin" (a sign of poor morals and lack of self-discipline). Card loans have higher status than consumer loans ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)). Interviews with young debtors aged 25–32 link debt to "classed shame" and to gendered adulthood milestones ([Birkeland & Vogt 2024](https://doi.org/10.18261/nwr.9.3.3), abstract-only). Advisers describe years of secrecy and the plea "ikke døm meg" (don't judge me). [VERIFIED or abstract, Moderate–High] **Implication.** Use a non-judgemental tone. Never shame, never normalise *taking on* debt ("alle gjør det", everyone does it), and offer a visible help path (NAV's free financial advice) that is separate from product sales. **Uncertainty.** Stigma is VERIFIED. Peer effects on card choice are INFERRED (Low–Moderate).

### Also relevant: anchoring and disclosure design

Showing a minimum payment pulls repayments down. At least 22% of near-minimum payers, or 9% of all accounts, respond in ways consistent with anchoring ([Keys & Wang](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22742)). Automatic minimum payments account for about 8% of all interest paid ([Sakaguchi et al. 2022](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/160903/)). Simpler disclosure improved recall and comparison without making loans more attractive ([KFST 2020](https://en.kfst.dk/publications/2020/20200619-consumers-benefit-from-simplified-information-disclosure)). Norway already defaults invoices to the full balance (Rundskriv 10/2016). Enkellån's credit-limit slider, however, **defaults to NOK 50,000**. That is an anchor sitting above the site's own advice to choose a low limit and above the NOK 30,000 regulatory exemption threshold [INFERRED, Moderate; anchoring on the limit is not tested in Norway]. A lower default should be tested.

## 9. Motivations and triggers: why people want a card is different from why they compare

### Why people want a card (background needs)

Norwegian evidence on the motive to **hold** a card is indirect, but it is consistent across sources. The motives are practical and transactional: travel and foreign use, safety online, insurance, loyalty and bonus schemes, phone wallets, a spare card, and work expenses. A minority of motives concern liquidity: unforeseen expenses, bridging a gap until payday, deposits, and consolidating small debts. SIFO 2019 found cards used for travel (44%), consumer goods (50%) and unforeseen expenses (30%), and found cards preferred because they are "lett å få tak i, brukervennlig og oppleves som trygt" (easy to get, user-friendly and experienced as safe) ([OsloMet/SIFO 2020](https://www.oslomet.no/forskning/forskningsnyheter/stor-forbrukslan-skam-i-norge)). *Bytteundersøkelsen* reasons and verbatims confirm the transactional cluster (Section 6). Finansportalen's design treats **foreign use, purchase volume and carrying a balance** as the usage patterns that decide which card is best ([Finansportalen](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/finansportalen/bank/kredittkort)). [VERIFIED, Moderate–High]

### Why people use a comparison service (a different motive)

The motive for *comparing* is price-led and risk-reducing. Forbrukerrådet frames comparison as paying less for exactly the same product ([27.04.2023](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/siste-nytt/en-av-fire-tror-ikke-det-er-noe-a-spare-pa-a-bytte-bank/)). UK research finds people use comparison tools to save time and money ([CMA Paper A](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/59c9356bed915d7bd5d75dda/paper-a-consumer-experiences.pdf), snippet-only). The CMA also identifies eligibility uncertainty as the central problem in card comparison ([RPC summary](https://rpclegal.com/thinking/tech/digital-comparison-tools-the-cmas-verdict), snippet-only). Yet comparison services are a **minority channel** in Norway. Of bank-service switchers in 2025, 55% simply contacted their own bank, and 27% used Finansportalen or another third-party service ([Bytte 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). Wanting to "see options without applying or being credit-checked" is plausible and is central to Enkellån's positioning, but no Norwegian consumer data measures it [HYPOTHESIS, Moderate].

### Channels

Measured channel use exists only for **bank services in general**, not for cards specifically. In 2025, switchers contacted their own bank (55%) or used its website (26%) far more often than Finansportalen (15%), renteradar.no (8%) or kredittkort.com (1%) ([Bytte 2026](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2026/05/bytteundersokelsen-2026-rapport.pdf)). The likely journey is non-linear [INFERRED, Moderate]:

1. A trigger.
2. The own bank or app.
3. A search engine.
4. Possibly a comparison site.
5. An application at the issuer, with its credit check.
6. The card arrives, and the old one may or may not be cancelled.

## 11. Decision criteria, concerns and objections

### Criteria differ by whether someone pays in full

There is **no independent Norwegian ranking of card attributes**. The closest stated-preference data concerns bank choice: loan rate (22%) and user-friendliness (22%) lead ([Finans Norge 2024](https://www.finansnorge.no/artikler/2024/02/bankkundene-er-aktive-som-aldri-for/), Tier 2). The best structural evidence is the set of attributes the public comparator chose to show: effective and nominal rate, annual fee, minimum income and age, credit limit, currency surcharge, purchase and withdrawal fees, travel and cancellation insurance, cashback and discounts, membership requirements, and minimum payment ([Finansportalen](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/finansportalen/bank/kredittkort)) [VERIFIED]. Most card balances are paid before interest accrues, which implies that **full payers weigh fees, currency surcharge, insurance and rewards, while revolvers should weigh effective rate and limit** [INFERRED, Moderate]. ### Why these five, and what they are not

The five composites cover the brief's three required contrasts. Ingrid, Erik and Marte are existing full payers. Camilla is an existing holder who revolves. Jonas is first-time. On orientation, Ingrid is benefit-focused, while Erik, Camilla, Jonas and Marte are cost-, flexibility- or limit-focused. **Transactor-type avatars outnumber revolver-type avatars three to one, deliberately.** That proportion mirrors the register's ratio of payers to borrowers. It does not estimate how many people fall into each segment.

The avatars overlap. Ingrid can become Camilla in a bad month. Erik's replacement decision can coincide with Marte's mortgage. Jonas becomes Erik or Ingrid within a few years. **These are evidence-informed composite personas, not statistically validated segments.** No segment size or percentage is claimed for any of them. Section 16 sets out the survey and latent-class analysis needed to test whether these clusters exist and how large they are.

## 13. Five detailed avatars

*Read this first. Each avatar is a composite built from the evidence above. The names and every detail marked "illustrative" are invented for readability. Personality is never inferred from age, gender or occupation. Every Norwegian hook is **proposed messaging, not a quotation**. Every ad built from these angles must also meet the compliance checklist in Section 15. The most important items are: the representative example shown in the frame and in the final frame; cost information at least as prominent as any benefit; Drive Media ApS named; and "sammenlign"/"du søker hos utstederen" (compare / you apply with the issuer) wording.*

### 13.1 Ingrid: the benefit-focused full payer

**A. Identity and lifestyle.** The age range supported by the evidence is **25–54**. The share of people with not-yet-due card balances rose most among 25–34-year-olds and peaks at about 60% among 45–54-year-olds ([Norges Bank 2026](https://www.norges-bank.no/bankplassen/arkiv/2026/dyrtid-og-hoye-renter-har-ikke-fort-til-storre-forbrukslan/)). Respondents who gave benefit-related reasons for opening a card were aged 29 to 56 ([Bytte 2024](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2024/06/rapport-bytteundersokelsen-2024.pdf); [2025](https://storage02.forbrukerradet.no/media/2025/05/bytteundersokelsen-2025.pdf)). The evidence supports her being in paid work only in the sense that issuers require a steady income. *Illustrative:* she travels abroad a few times a year. Her household situation is not evidenced.

**B. Credit card usage.** Ingrid is an existing cardholder who pays the full balance by the due date. One survey respondent describes the pattern as "Bruker kredittkort til forbruk og betaler på betalingsfrist" (I use the credit card for spending and pay by the due date). She holds the card for its travel terms, insurance, bonus or loyalty points and discounts. "Gode vilkår ved kjøp av reise, tilbud i butikker" (good terms on travel and shop offers) was the top reason for opening a card in the 2023 and 2024 data (56% and 59%). ## 15. Meta advertising implications: let the creative choose the audience, and keep cost at the front

### Ground rules every ad must meet

Meta lists Norway among the countries where credit-card ads, including **"brand ads for credit cards, regardless of offer"**, must use the Financial products and services special ad category ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/567423788405762); [scope](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1157846251802527)). In that category gender is fixed to all, detailed-targeting exclusions are removed, and the location radius goes to the required minimum. Age is normally fixed at 18–65+, but credit advertisers in Europe "can select a different age range in order to meet their industry and local requirements" ([Meta developer docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category)). The table below sets out the Norwegian legal requirements alongside Meta's.

### Testing framework

**What to vary.** Change one variable at a time: the territory; the hook framing (question or statement; for cost, "you pay" or "you avoid paying"); the format (static, carousel, motion, AI video); the CTA (no-sign-up list or sign-up); and the prominence of the disclosure. **Every variant must itself be compliant.** Never test a non-compliant variant to "see what happens".

| Outcome stage | Definition | Data source | Can show | Cannot show |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery and engagement | Impressions, thumb-stop rate, click-through | Meta | Attention | Comprehension, intent, whether the avatar is right |
| **Comparison visit** | Arrival on /kredittkort or /kredittkort/tilbud | Site analytics (UTM, fbclid, already captured) | Interest in comparing | Eligibility or demand for a card |
| No-sign-up list view | Use of "se kortene uten å melde deg på" | Site | Low-commitment comparison | Later intent |
| **Sign-up** | Step-1 submit with consent | Form | Willingness to receive marketing | Demand for a card |
| Step-2 completion | Questionnaire submitted | Form | Engagement with the filter | Approval odds |
| **Provider click** | Outbound "Søk her" click through the redirect | Redirect logs | Intent to go to an issuer | An application |
| **Application** | Started or submitted at the partner | Partner postback (aggregate) | Demand | Approval |
| **Approval** | Card granted | Partner (aggregate) | A successful match | Whether the card suited the user |
| Quality and harm signals | Unsubscribes, consent withdrawals, complaints, "Vis alle" (show all) use, help-link clicks | Email/SMS platform, site | Fairness of the journey | Causation without a control |

**What cannot be concluded from engagement.** Several inferences are not supported by engagement data:

- **Creative differences are not clean message effects.** Meta's algorithm delivers each creative to different people, and under the special ad category the audience cannot be held constant by targeting, so differences mix message and audience. Use split tests where available, and read results as "creative plus the audience it attracted".
- **Engagement is not comprehension.** Whether viewers understood the representative example or the company's role has to be tested separately (Section 16).
- **Clicks do not validate avatars.** Validating them needs an optional on-site self-identification question or the survey in Section 16.
- **Sign-ups measure consent to marketing,** and Enkellån's own terms make them a marketing list. They are not card demand.
- **Funnel stages are not interchangeable.** Provider clicks are not applications, and applications are not approvals.
- **Leads cannot size segments.** Leads from ads are self-selected.

Set sample sizes, stopping rules and multiple-comparison corrections **in advance** from observed baseline rates. This report contains none, because no Norwegian baseline exists in the evidence. **Measurement privacy:** keep the verified practice that "nothing financial (credit limit, income, payment remarks) is ever pushed to the dataLayer", and extend it to Meta's pixel and Conversions API.

## 16. Limitations, evidence gaps and a validation plan

### Limitations

This review rests on a thin evidence base for its central question. **No Norwegian dataset describes the people who compare credit cards**: their repayment behaviour, their channels or their decision criteria. The avatars are built by combining population register data, an annual switching survey with small subgroups (N≈145–336 for the credit questions), short verbatims, and professional testimony from a selected high-severity clientele. Several key sources were seen only as snippets or institutional summaries, including SIFO's full reports, Birkeland & Vogt, Kempson & Poppe, the NHH theses, current Forbrukertilsynet web guidance, and the Storting and CCD2 documents. Some evidence is old: invoicing data from before 2013, a 2015 survey of minors, and a 2014 survey on online payment. International behavioural evidence was read at abstract level, and it comes from markets with more revolving and looser rules than Norway. Enkellån's pages describe how the company positions itself, not how consumers respond. Finally, **no new primary research was conducted for this report.** Everything below is a plan.

### Quantitative survey

**Target population and sampling.** The population is adults aged 18 and over resident in Norway. Recruit from a large national panel with quotas on age, gender, region and education, and weight to SSB population figures. This is the approach Forbrukerrådet's own survey uses, with an Ipsos/Norstat panel of about 108,000. Prefer a probability-recruited panel if one is available. Run a short screener on a large base, then give the full questionnaire to a stratified sample with a **boost of 18–29-year-olds**.

**Screeners that separate the four outcomes.** S1 asks about holding a personal credit card in one's own name, distinct from store accounts and BNPL **[OWN]**. S2 asks about the number of cards and the banded total limit, with a band boundary at NOK 30,000 **[OWN]**. S3 asks how often the card was used for payment in the last 3 months **[USE]**. S4 asks how many times in the last 12 months the full bill was not paid by the due date (0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6–11 / every month / don't know) **[REV]**. S5 asks what was usually paid in those months (the minimum, more than the minimum, it varies) **[REV]**. S6 asks whether the respondent looked into or compared credit cards in the last 12 months, and with what outcome **[ACQ]**. S7 asks whether the respondent has ever held a card, and whether they are considering one in the next 6 months **[ACQ, first-time]**.

**Sample size reasoning.** At 95% confidence the margin of error is ±1.96·√(p(1−p)/n). For p = 0.5 that gives **±3.1 pp at n = 1,000, ±4.9 pp at n = 400 and ±6.9 pp at n = 200**. A rough planning assumption for incidence multiplies 15% of card users searching the market by 61% household holding, giving about 9% of adults. That is researcher arithmetic mixing household and individual bases, and the screener's own measured incidence should replace it. Reaching about 600 recent comparers would mean screening roughly 6,500–7,000 adults. A full sample of about 1,500 (about 600 recent comparers, 600 cardholders who did not compare, and 300 non-holders including people considering a card) supports reading comparers to about ±4 pp and leaves room for latent class analysis. All of these figures are design targets, not estimates of the market.

**Segmentation analysis.** Run **latent class analysis** on behavioural indicators: repayment frequency, the card's job, trigger, channel, MaxDiff-derived criteria, knowledge, self-efficacy, resilience and mortgage proximity. Use demographics only as covariates. Choose the number of classes by BIC, entropy, bootstrap likelihood-ratio tests and interpretability. Validate on a holdout sample, cross-tabulate the classes against the five a-priori avatars, and **report class sizes only with confidence intervals and only from this new data**. **Limitations and biases:** opt-in panels under-represent people with low literacy and people in financial distress; shame (57%) biases self-reported revolving downwards, so use behavioural and non-judgemental wording; 12-month recall introduces error; wording effects can be large, as the 76%→61% shift showed; and household and individual bases differ.

### Qualitative interviews

**Participant groups**, about 6–8 interviews each, roughly 30–40 in total. Saturation should be judged rather than fixed in advance.

1. First-time researchers aged 18–29 who looked into a card in the last 6 months.
2. Full payers who added or replaced a card in the last 12 months, mixing benefit and practical triggers.
3. Cardholders who did not pay in full in at least 3 of the last 12 months and are not in arrears. Screen with neutral wording and follow a distress protocol.
4. People who hold a card and have applied for, or plan to apply for, a mortgage within 12 months.
5. People who started a comparison or application and abandoned it.

**Recruitment:** use an independent recruiter, not Enkellån's marketing list, unless participants give separate research consent. Exclude employees of banks, brokers and marketing agencies. Pay incentives.

**Discussion guide (non-leading).**

- Warm-up about money routines, with no products named.
- Open the main section with: "Tell me about the last time you got, changed or looked into a credit card – start from the first moment you thought about it."
- Neutral probes: "What happened next?", "What did you look at?", "Was there a point where you nearly stopped? What was going on?", "What mattered?" Never ask leading questions such as "Was the rate important?"
- Ask how they would explain to a friend what their card is for, and how they would work out what it costs (teach-back).
- Ask whom they trust for information.
- Show stimulus only at the end: Enkellån's pages and the ad concepts. Use comprehension tasks, not preference ones: "What happens if you click?", "Who is behind this?", "What does this example mean?"

**Decision-journey reconstruction:** timeline mapping using artefacts participants choose to share (screenshots, emails, browser history), the critical-incident technique, an attribute card sort, and a think-aloud replay of their search. **Ethics:** informed consent, data minimisation, no collection of debt-register consents or data, and a signpost to NAV's free financial advice.

### Behavioural validation

The research would combine five kinds of test, with hypotheses and decision rules pre-registered and ethics review before any live test.

1. **Moderated usability tests** of the Enkellån flow, in rounds of about 5–8 participants per key group (a UX convention, not a statistical claim). Tasks: find the lowest effective rate; say what you agree to when you sign up; find who runs the site and how the order is set; reach the list without signing up.
2. **Unmoderated comprehension tests** of ad frames, using 5-second and first-click tests. Can viewers identify the representative example, the company and the action (compare or apply)?
3. **Consented journey analytics** across the funnel stages in Section 15. Analyse outbound clicks by list position against rate, to see whether the European finding on rank-position effects holds for Enkellån.
4. **On-site A/B tests**, with ethics review, of:
   - a lower slider default against NOK 50,000, measuring the requested limit;
   - a sort-by-effective-rate option, measuring the rate of the chosen listing;
   - split, optional consents, measuring sign-ups, complaints and unsubscribes.
5. **Aggregated partner-reported data** on applications and approvals.

---

## Conclusion: the trustworthy message is also the one the evidence supports

The evidence changes the starting assumption behind most credit-card advertising. The typical Norwegian card comparer is probably not a borrower hunting for a low rate. More likely it is a full payer whose card has just stopped fitting a trip, a phone wallet, a bank switch or a mortgage plan. For that person, the most useful facts are the dull, verifiable ones Enkellån already states but does not lead with:

- comparing is not applying;
- the whole limit counts when a bank assesses you;
- bonuses pay only if you pay in full;
- here is who runs this list and how it is paid.

These facts are also what Norwegian law requires to be prominent, so persuasion and compliance pull in the same direction rather than against each other.

The bigger risk is not the advertising but the product behind it. A commission-influenced order that puts the cheapest rate fifth, missing currency, withdrawal and minimum-payment fields, mandatory multi-channel marketing consent, a NOK 50,000 slider default, and inconsistent rate examples would each undermine a trust-led campaign as soon as a sceptical user clicks through. The fastest gains are therefore product fixes, which can be tested at low risk before any media spend scales:

- a sort-by-rate option;
- the missing cost fields;
- separate, optional consents;
- a lower default limit;
- reconciled examples;
- an educational cost page with a help link.

A social-media ban on credit marketing remains under political consideration. Building that trust now is also the best hedge against losing the channel.
