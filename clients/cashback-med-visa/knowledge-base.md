# Cashback med Visa (CMV) — Client Knowledge Base

Consolidated from the five strategy documents (Aug 2026) plus a full review of cashbackmedvisa.dk (6 Nov 2026).
Purpose: a single source of truth to build B2B (merchant-acquisition) ads from.

---

## 1. What CMV is, in one paragraph

A **card-linked cashback programme** operated by **Loyalty Key A/S** (Dampfærgevej 21, 4., 2100 København Ø) in partnership with Visa. Official product name on site: **"Cashback i partnerskab med Visa"** (logo lock-up: "Cashback | VISA"). Consumers register their Visa cards once and automatically get money back when they pay at participating merchants. **Merchants are the paying customer**: they fund the cashback and pay CMV to be marketed to the member base. Our ads sell the **merchant partnership** (B2B), with the consumer experience as the proof mechanism.

> Working definition: a customer-acquisition and loyalty partnership for merchants that uses CMV's consumer network and card-linked rewards to generate purchases and repeat behaviour.

**Marketing channels included**

## 4. Business objective & strategy north star

- Target: **75–100 B2B leads/month**, but management cares more about **lead quality, deal size and partner commercial potential**.
- Current strong fit: restaurants/cafés, salons/beauty, local retail (SMB).
- Desired growth: **national chains/multi-location and especially e-commerce**.
- E-commerce prospects are harder: digitally sophisticated, and they challenge attribution ("would it have happened anyway?").
- **Thesis:** *CMV should not try to look like the biggest loyalty programme. It should look like the most commercially accountable one.*
- **Messaging shift:** from "Get access to 400,000 consumers" → to "What happened after we reached them?"

|---|---|---|
| CPL | 784 DKK | 513 DKK |
| Junk/not relevant | 14.1% | 53.7% |
| Qualified leads | 73 | 19 |

| CAC / won partner | **4,802 DKK** | **23,862 DKK** |

→ **Never judge on CPL alone.** KPI ladder: CPL → cost per qualified lead → cost per meeting → CAC.

**Heyflow funnel:** 1,322 visitors → 29 submits = **2.19%**. **82.1% mobile.** First step: 1,290 views / 1,134 exits = **88% drop-off**. Name/contact step: 65% drop-off. Treat this as an **ad-to-funnel promise mismatch** to investigate, not automatically a form problem. The Heyflow qualifies on industry, revenue, region, transaction volume, employees, existing loyalty programme and Visa acceptance, then shows "how many CMV users could become your customers."

**Creative history**
- **Koncept_3A = current control.** 30-day sample: ~34% of spend, 20 of 40 leads, 6.8% click-to-lead vs 3.7% blended. Summary doc, a different window: 32 leads @ 578 DKK. Direct, merchant-relevant.
- **Koncept_1B** — the "wasted ad spend / paying for results" tension resonates. Keep it as a control-style message.
- **Heart_of_Nature-Lang** — 1.13% link CTR, 42% hook rate, but 3.16% hold and only 1.7% form completion. **Attention without qualification.** Unusual storytelling stops the scroll but needs faster commercial relevance.
- 14 of 25 ads had zero leads on ~5,785 DKK combined. Low-spend rows are directional only.

**Sales/ops leakage (not a creative problem):** 27 paid leads never contacted; median first contact ~35.1 h.

**Measurement gaps:** Facebook Lead ID is empty on all 179 HubSpot deals, so there's no creative-level CAC. Revenue, closed-lost reason and firmographic fields are incomplete.

## 6. Why partners churn (the objections our ads must respect)

From 96 partners / 181 locations with documented churn reasons:

| Reason | Locations | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Doesn't believe sales are incremental** | 93 | **51%** |
| Too expensive relative to value | 29 | 16% |
| Poor expectation-setting | 17 | 9% |
| Missing performance | 10 | 6% |
| Own programme / strategic change | 7 | 4% |

- Restaurants: 61 locations in the sample, 37 coded incrementality. **This is share of churn, not churn rate.** No active-base denominator.
- Beauty/salons and local retail: price-to-value is relatively more prominent.
- Roughly two-thirds of documented churn = partner unconvinced by the economic value.

## 7. Consumer voice (reviews: proof material and risk)

- Ratings: **Trustpilot 4.1/5 (237 reviews)**, **App Store 4.3/5 (~6,000 ratings)**, **Google Play 4.2/5 (~1.41K, 100K+ downloads)**. Replies to 95% of negative reviews, usually within 24h.
- Strongest positive themes: **set-and-forget simplicity**, support quality, **surprise/delight of money back**.
- Friction themes: missing cashback, Visa/Dankort routing, re-registering new cards, MobilePay, CVV/phishing fear, payout friction.
- **Key quotes**
  - "You don't have to do anything. Just use the card as usual." → the consumer proposition
  - **"I've started choosing the stores with cashback."** → **the reason merchants should buy**
  - Another user earned 3,000+ DKK and actively seeks cashback shops
  - **"I've earned DKK 1,900 on purchases I would have made anyway."** → **the reason merchants churn**
- **Core creative insight:** consumer story = "free money with almost no effort". Merchant story must become **"customers who choose you because of CMV, and evidence that proves it."**

## 8. Competitive frame

- Direct: **Visma LogBuy, Forbrugsforeningen, PlusKort**. LogBuy and PlusKort can claim similar or larger reach, so **reach is not a defensible differentiator**.
- Budget alternatives: **Meta, Google, TikTok**, affiliates, delivery platforms, discounting, booking platforms, email/SMS, retail media.
- CMV's differentiators to lean on: card-linked (no codes or cards to remember), payment-verified transactions, sales-based model, targeting by real spend data + postcode, dashboard with repeat-customer metrics, Visa association, human Partner Team.

## 10. Concept system (5 creative mechanisms)

| Concept | Mechanism | Hook | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Start at the Sale** | Reverse storytelling | "This is where most marketing reports stop. We start here." Open on a payment, rewind: purchase ← visit ← CMV offer ← push/app | E-com, multi-location, adaptable to restaurants |
| **2. A Funeral for Clicks** | Humour / pattern interrupt | "Today we mourn the clicks that never bought anything." → payment beep → "Clicks are nice. Customers are better." | E-com + broad |
| **3. The 4,500 DKK Challenge** | Radical transparency | "4,500 DKK. So what would we have to prove for that to be worth it?" | Local owners, e-com, multi-location |
| **4. Please Don't Become Our Customer** | Reverse psychology | "Please don't become a Cashback med Visa partner." Drops impressions/clicks/reach cards → reveals a receipt → addresses 4,500 DKK | Restaurants + local service/retail |
| **5. 3 Uncomfortable Questions** | Open-loop interrogation | "Before you give us 4,500 kroner, ask these three questions." Q1/3 new business? Q2/3 what happens after purchase one? Q3/3 why 4,500 DKK? | E-com + multi-location |

**Static message tests per avatar (static-first)**
- **Restaurant:** "Tuesday at 18:30. Twelve empty tables. What is each one worth to you?" · "More bookings are good. Additional bookings are better." · "Would they have chosen you anyway?"
- **Local service/retail:** "An empty 14:00 appointment earns exactly 0 DKK." · "What is one repeat customer worth to your business?" · "Why pay for marketing without knowing what it sold?" (1B-style control)
- **E-commerce (carousel candidate):** "Would they have bought anyway?" · "Your next growth channel shouldn't end at the click." · "Don't buy reach. Demand a business case."
- **Multi-location:** "One growth channel. Every location." · "400,000 consumers isn't the point. What they do next is." · "Can your growth channel prove what happened after the reach?"

**Static versions of the concepts:** "Your next growth channel shouldn't end at the click." / "RIP to clicks that never bought anything." / "4,500 DKK. What should we have to prove for that to be worth it?" / "Please don't become our partner. Not if you're happy paying for marketing without knowing what it actually sold." / "Before you give us 4,500 DKK, ask these 3 questions."

**Scale path:** local-demand winners → Start at the Sale / Please Don't. Price/value winners → 4,500 DKK Challenge. E-com proof winners → Start at the Sale / 3 Questions. Clicks-vs-sales winners → Funeral for Clicks.

## 11. Testing & decision rules

- Wave 1: 2–3 statics per avatar. Keep audience, CTA, form and visual system constant so the **message** is the variable. **Koncept 3A and 1B stay as controls.**
- Read signals:
  - High CTR + strong completion/quality → scale into video, test 2 hook variants
  - High CTR + weak completion → hook works but doesn't qualify; tighten body/proof/landing
  - Low CTR → rewrite or pause before production spend
  - **Higher CPL + better qualified leads → do NOT kill**
- Read order: spend → CTR → click-to-lead → CPL → qualified rate/cost → meetings → CAC.
- Don't kill on tiny samples. Don't claim creative-level CAC (no Lead ID). Separate sales-ops leaks from creative problems.

## 12. Claim discipline & compliance (must-follow)

- Only claim "new customers", "incremental sales" or uplift where CMV can substantiate it. **Where proof is incomplete, ask the question rather than claim the answer.**
- "Measurable" ≠ "incremental". Reporting a transaction doesn't prove causality.
- Avoid absolute "always automatic / nothing can go wrong" (MobilePay, Visa/Dankort routing and new-card exceptions exist).
- 400K reach = supporting proof after the business case, never the headline idea.
- **Expectation-setting risk spotted on the site:** "salgsbaseret uden faste omkostninger" sits next to a 4,500 DKK startup fee, and the 12-month minimum isn't shown. Ads should be honest about cost and commitment, since 9% of churn is poor expectation-setting. The site also says "helt automatisk" (fully automatic).
- Inconsistency on site: hero says 2,000+ shops, FAQ says "flere hundrede" (several hundred). Use **2,000+** consistently, as on the homepage and partner page.
- Dashboard mock-up numbers are illustrative. Use the visual, not the figures.
- Route claims about Visa, reach, incrementality, pricing and partner results through brand/Visa compliance before production.
