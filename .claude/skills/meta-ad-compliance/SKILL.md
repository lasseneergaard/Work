## 1. The core rule: Meta reviews meaning, not keywords

Meta's review combines machine-learning classifiers with human reviewers. Both judge **what the ad promises and what the funnel sells**, not individual words. That has two consequences:

1. **A synonym of a banned claim is still banned.** "Guaranteed profit", "can't lose", "risk-free income" and "money on autopilot" are all the same violation.
2. **Swapping words to hide the product is circumvention.** Examples: writing "markets" so the ad isn't detected as trading, misspelling words ("tr@ding", "f0rex"), hiding a broker funnel behind an "education" ad, or cloaking the landing page. Meta penalises this harder than a normal rejection, with ad account, Business Manager or personal profile bans. **Never do it.** The brand name "FX Trading Vision" appears in every logo anyway, so hiding the topic can't work.

**Smart wording that is allowed** means choosing the accurate, lower-hype way to say something true:

| Hype version (risky) | Accurate version (use) |
|---|---|
| Copy my trades | See my analysis / Learn how I plan each trade |
| Free signals that win | Free daily market insights |
| Trade like a pro | Learn from an experienced trader |
| Make money trading | Learn how the markets work |
| Join 200k winners | Join 200,000+ members (only if true) |
| No experience needed, just copy | Beginner-friendly lessons |
| Quit your job | Learn at your own pace |
| Trades that actually work | Every setup explained |

"Markets", "market insights" and "trade ideas" are fine to use, **as long as the product really is what the ad says it is**.

## 2. Two legal routes for FX Trading Vision

Decide which route a campaign is on before writing any copy.

**Route A: education (no verification needed, if true)**
Meta allows "information about financial education" and "educational advertisements about a financial service or product that does not provide the ability to obtain or connect with that product or service" without financial-services verification.
- Copy must sell **learning**: lessons, analysis, community, explanations.
- The **landing page must be educational too**. It must not open a broker account, connect a copy-trading tool or show "start copying" as the first step. If the funnel does that, the campaign is on Route B, whatever the ad says.

**Route B: financial service (copy trading, signals, broker sign-up, account management)**
- "Investment products and opportunities" need **advertiser verification** for audiences in: **Australia, Hong Kong, India, Ireland, Israel, Spain, Taiwan, Thailand, United Kingdom, United States**. Some countries require a licence (e.g. AFSL in Australia, FCA authorisation in the UK). Without it, ads to those countries get rejected.
- **US audience or US-based advertiser:** the **Financial Products and Services special ad category** is mandatory (since 21 Jan 2025). Not choosing it is a rejection reason. It also restricts targeting by age, gender, ZIP code and detailed targeting.
- Route B copy can mention copying or signals, but all of sections 3 to 6 still apply.

When unsure which route applies, ask the user what the landing page does after the click.

## 3. Banned claim types, with equivalents that are equally banned

| Banned claim type | Examples (all equally banned) |
|---|---|
| **Specific earnings or returns** | "$2,598 last week", "made €10k", "+340%", "5 figures a month", "pay your rent in a week" |
| **Win rates and accuracy** | "85%+ win rate", "9 out of 10 trades win", "accurate signals", "never wrong", "sniper entries" |
| **Guarantees or no risk** | "guaranteed", "risk-free", "can't lose", "safe profits", "zero risk", "protected gains" |
| **Effortless or passive money** | "money on autopilot", "passive income", "get paid while you sleep", "copy and profit", "one tap to profit" |
| **Get rich quick or life change** | "quit your job", "financial freedom in 30 days", "become a millionaire", "change your life overnight" |
| **Implied results** | "copy the same trades" next to results, "trades that actually work", "proven to make money", "verified profits" (unless audited by a named third party) |
| **Urgency or scarcity hype** | "last chance", "only 10 spots", "before it's too late", "secret strategy banks don't want you to know" |
| **Personal attributes (Meta policy)** | "Are you broke?", "Tired of being poor?", "You're losing money", "Still stuck in your 9-5?" — never say or imply what the viewer's finances or situation are |
| **Aggressive or violent framing** | "weapon", "the wolf of…", "kill the market", "destroy your job" |
| **Fake or unverifiable social proof** | invented member counts, "5-star reviews" counts, ratings that aren't the current live score |

## 4. Imagery rules

**Never use:**
- P&L, balance, payout or broker screenshots, real or mocked. AI-generated screenshots with garbled text are an instant red flag.
- Cash stacks, flying banknotes, cheques, gold bars
- Luxury as the reward: supercars, yachts, private jets, watches in focus, mansion pools. Rule of thumb: if the image says "you'll get this", remove it. A city skyline or balcony background is fine.
- Sexualised imagery: bikinis, cleavage or body focus. Crop to head and shoulders or use a different frame.
- Before/after lifestyle comparisons
- Meta/Facebook/Instagram logos or UI

**Safe:** the presenter talking to camera, desk or laptop setups, charts as *education* (no P&L), phone showing lessons or analysis, community or event photos, neutral city backgrounds.

## 5. Required elements

- **Risk line on the image or in the primary text** (on-image preferred for this vertical):
  - Route A: `Educational content, not financial advice. Trading involves risk of loss.`
  - Route B: `Trading involves significant risk of loss and is not suitable for everyone. Past performance is not indicative of future results.` For UK/EU audiences of CFD products, add the broker's ESMA line: `XX% of retail investor accounts lose money when trading CFDs with this provider.`
- **Every factual claim must be true today:** Trustpilot score, member count, "free". If joining is free but trading needs a funded broker account, the ad or landing page must say so.
- **Age targeting 18+.**
- **Landing page matches the ad.** Same offer, same claims level, risk warning visible, no earnings claims on the page.

## 6. Approved phrase bank

Headlines: `Learn the Markets` · `Learn From Mike` · `Free Market Insights` · `Learn Together` · `Your Phone. Your Trading Classroom.` · `Join FX Trading Vision Free` · `Daily Market Analysis, Explained`

Sublines: `Daily market analysis, explained step by step` · `See how Mike plans every trade idea` · `Every setup explained` · `Learn at your own pace` · `Beginner-friendly lessons` · `Learn in 20 minutes a day` · `Forex • Commodities • Indices`

Proof (only if true): `★ 4.9 on Trustpilot` · `200,000+ members` · `Free to join`

CTAs: `Join the Free Community` · `Start Learning` · `Join FX Trading Vision` · `See How It Works`

Words to use with care (fine in context, risky when stacked with results): `copy`, `signals`, `profit`, `win`, `free trades`, `no experience needed`, `verified`, `expert`, `professional`.

## 7. Pre-flight checklist (run on every ad before delivering it)

1. Route A or B decided, and the landing page matches the route?
2. Any number tied to money, returns or win rate? → remove
3. Any phrase in section 3, or something that means the same? → rewrite with section 1's accurate column
4. Does any line say or imply something about the viewer's money, job or life? → rewrite
5. Image: P&L or screenshot, cash, luxury-as-reward, sexualised framing? → remove, crop or replace
6. Risk line present?
7. Every proof point true today?
8. Targeting countries need verification (section 2)? US → special ad category?
9. Would the ad still be honest if a regulator read it next to the landing page?

When delivering ads, include a short compliance note listing what was checked and any open assumptions (e.g. "4.9★ assumed current", "landing page assumed educational").
